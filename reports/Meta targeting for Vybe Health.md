# Creative, not interest lists, targets Vybe's buyers

Meta ad targeting for Vybe Health should be built around creative personas and on-platform engagement audiences, not around interest stacks, because in 2026 Meta treats detailed targeting, lookalikes, gender and maximum age as suggestions for every performance goal Vybe would plausibly use (profile visits, leads, landing-page views, link clicks), and because Meta's own WHOOP case study found broad targeting cut cost per purchase 29% versus segmented audiences (Meta-official, page not independently re-opened in this research). The buyers of WHOOP and Oura alternatives are a 25–44 core split by gender and motive: WHOOP is ~55–57% male and performance-framed, Oura is 59–72% female and sleep/cycle-framed, and the "subscription-fatigued" buyer is defined by behaviour (lapsed or price-sensitive WHOOP/Garmin owners, of whom only 32% of US wearable owners carry any subscription) rather than by any demographic Meta can select. Fitness-activity interests such as CrossFit, Running, Weight training and Garmin Fitness still exist; competitor-brand interests (WHOOP, Oura, Polar, Amazfit) could not be verified; health-condition interests, detailed-targeting exclusions and condition-named custom audiences are gone. The January 2025 health & wellness data restrictions matter less than feared for a pre-launch account, because the events it would optimise to (ThruPlay, profile visits, instant-form leads, landing-page views) are the ones that survive even a Tier 2 restriction, provided the landing domain, URLs, event names and audience names never carry condition words. Competitively, WHOOP and Oura run large static "direct offer" libraries (75% and 64% direct-offer hooks respectively), Amazfit spends its Meta budget on HYROX-branded smartwatches rather than the Helio Strap, and no tracked brand puts the "no subscription, own your data" message in front of Meta audiences, which leaves Vybe's "own and create" position unpurchased. If the founder later approves any spend, the evidence supports at most two campaigns under $5K/month (an engagement/profile-visit seeding campaign with one broad US 18+ ad set and 6–12 conceptually distinct Reels, plus a small instant-form lead campaign built on a guide or quiz rather than a "waitlist"), measured by Meta's Instagram-follows column, follow rate per ad, cost per ThruPlay, saves, DMs and cost per lead. This report is a reference for that decision; no ads are approved and nothing here is a recommendation to spend.

## The 25–44 core splits by gender and motive, and the open buyer is behavioural

Every recovery-wearable brand with published data has its centre of gravity at ages 25–44, and the two incumbents have partitioned that bracket by gender and message. Oura disclosed that its base "shifted from primarily male to 59 percent who identify as female" (company-reported, [Future FemHealth](https://www.futurefemhealth.com/p/oura-reveals-its-users-are-now-predominantly)), its chief commercial officer said the fastest-growing segment is "women in their early twenties" and that Oura is "deliberately letting gym rats slip away to Whoop" (company-reported via press, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/oura-is-winning-young-women-and-losing-gym-rats-and-its-fine-with-that/)), and a secondary report on its 2026 IPO filing puts women at roughly **72% of members** with international markets under 20% of hardware sales (company filing via secondary source, unverified against the S-1, [Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/oura-oura-ipo-smart-ring-maker-files-for-nasdaq-with-1-4b-revenue-and-5m-subscribers/)). WHOOP is the mirror image: whoop.com's visitor panel runs **57% male** with 25–34 the largest group (estimate, web-traffic panel, [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/whoop.com/)), yet its CMO said in September 2026 that women are now "approximately one in four new Whoop members globally" and that membership among women 40+ "has tripled since January 2025" (company-reported via press, [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/09/21/whoop-sees-strong-sales-growth-from-women-in-the-middle-east/)). The widely repeated claim that over 70% of US WHOOP members earn $100K+ could not be traced to any primary source and should be treated as unverified ([CanvasBusinessModel](https://canvasbusinessmodel.com/blogs/target-market/whoop-target-market)). The only quantified income proxy for the hybrid athlete is older CrossFit market data (average income ~$150,000, 86% degree-holders, largest bracket 25–34 at 40%), which is pre-2020 and estimate-grade ([Rally Fitness](https://rallyfitness.com/blogs/news/16063884-latest-crossfit-market-research-data)).

These profiles map well onto Meta's US audiences. Pew's 2025 survey finds Instagram used by **80% of 18–29s and 62% of 30–49s**, and by 55% of women versus 44% of men (survey, [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/)); ad-reach data put US Instagram at 28.3% aged 25–34 and 19.4% aged 35–44, and US Facebook at 25.9% aged 25–34 with women at 53% (estimate from Meta ad-planning data, [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/398166/us-instagram-user-age-distribution/); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/187549/facebook-distribution-of-users-age-group-usa/)). Rock Health's 2025 Census-matched survey of 8,000 adults finds 46% of US adults own a wearable, Gen Z ownership at 66%, owners "younger, wealthier, more urban," and, most usefully for Vybe, that **only 32% of wearable owners have any attached subscription** (survey, [Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/insights/whats-your-score-insights-on-wearables-and-connected-devices-from-rock-healths-2025-consumer-adoption-survey/)). That last figure is the strongest structural evidence that a buy-once band's addressable pool is far larger than the WHOOP-plus-Oura installed base.

The subscription-fatigued buyer, however, has no survey behind them. No study quantifying WHOOP or Oura cancellation intent was found. The evidence is event-driven: WHOOP's May 2025 $49 upgrade-fee reversal after "a huge user backlash" (press, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/11/fitness-tracker-whoop-faces-unhappy-customers-over-upgrade-policy)), reviewer arithmetic that "$25–$40/month indefinitely" means "$600–$960 and own nothing" after two years (review, [Dan Curtis](https://dancurtisgo.substack.com/p/i-wore-an-apple-watch-oura-whoop)), Zepp Health's admission that it "didn't manufacture enough of the Helio Strap to cater for the Q3 and Q4 high seasons" (company-reported, [Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-zepp-healths-q4-2025-revenue-surges-43-93CH-4561628)), and a TechRadar poll in which **80% of 1,325 Garmin users never used Connect+** (survey, self-selected, [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/ill-switch-to-another-brand-i-polled-over-1-000-garmin-users-to-see-what-they-thought-of-garmins-connect-premium-tier-one-year-after-its-launch)). Oura's counter-signal is 12-month retention "in the high 80s" (company-reported, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/oura-is-winning-young-women-and-losing-gym-rats-and-its-fine-with-that/)), which implies fatigue is concentrated among WHOOP-style performance users, not Oura-style wellness users. The behavioural definition matters for Meta: none of it is selectable as an interest, so it has to be expressed in creative and captured as engagement audiences.

HYROX racers, Vybe's hard community target, overlap almost exactly with WHOOP's core. The sport grew from 570,000 participants in 2024/25 to about **1.5 million in 2025/26** with a 2-million target for 2026/27 (company-reported, [SportsPro](https://www.sportspro.com/features/finance-investment/hyrox-business-model-mass-participation-private-equity-investment/); [endurance.biz](https://endurance.biz/2026/industry-news/targeting-2-million-athletes-hyrox-expands-global-calendar-for-2026-27-season/)); the largest age brackets are 25–29 and 30–34 (estimate from results data, [HyroxDataLab](https://hyroxdatalab.com/articles/hyrox-season-8-trends)); a 418-person survey gives a mean age of 37.4 with 61.5% from strength backgrounds (survey, preprint, [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.09.26359590v1.full.pdf)); and women are roughly 38% of competitors (estimate, [Gym-Flooring stats hub](https://www.gym-flooring.com/blogs/stats-hub/hyrox-stats)). No HYROX income data or wearable-brand share exists. Life-situation segments are thinner still: cycle and perimenopause tracking is the one life-stage driver with hard evidence (Oura built its female majority on it, company-reported, [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250812718849/en/URA-Advances-Womens-Health-with-First-of-its-Kind-Support-for-Pregnancy-and-Menopause-Bridging-Gaps-in-Care-with-Personalized-Biometrics-and-Clinical-Integrations)); new parents and shift-working nurses have academic wearable studies but no market sizing (academic, [The Lancet eBioMedicine](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/ebiom/article/PIIS2352-3964(25)00084-2/fulltext); [JMIR Nursing](https://doi.org/10.2196/96912)); longevity buyers are estimated at 35–55 and ~45% female (market-research estimate, [GlobeNewswire/Astute Analytica](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/09/3234305/0/en/biohacking-market-to-reach-us-216-68-billion-by-2035-driven-by-metabolic-monitoring-wearable-diagnostics-and-personalized-health-optimization-astute-analytica.html)), with WHOOP's 350,000-person Advanced Labs waitlist as the hard demand signal (company-reported, [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/whoop-expands-advanced-labs-offering-non-members-adds-grails-multi-cancer-detection)).

## Meta's 2026 toolbox: a few hard fences, many suggestions, some bans

The single most consequential fact for Vybe's targeting design is that detailed targeting is no longer a constraint for the goals it would use. Jon Loomer documented in February 2026 that Advantage+ detailed targeting cannot be turned off for eleven performance goals, including maximise conversions, landing page views, link clicks, leads, and **Instagram profile and Facebook Page visits**; "it does not matter if you turn Advantage+ off, switch to original audiences… detailed targeting will only be used as suggestions" (practitioner documenting Meta UI, [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/detailed-targeting-suggestion/)). Lookalikes became forced suggestions for nine goals in late 2025 (same source). Meta's own product page describes Advantage+ audience as taking "your usual audience selections, such as a customer list, a gender, or an interest and expands beyond them," prioritising matches before "searching more widely" (Meta-official, [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/meta-advantage-plus/audience)); at the account level the only hard controls are a minimum age of 18–25, geo exclusions and placement exclusions, with no maximum age (Meta-official, [Marketing API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/advantage-shopping-campaigns/)). The goals Loomer's list omits (video views/ThruPlay, reach, post engagement) can still hard-constrain an audience, which makes a video-views campaign the one place a manual fitness audience still functions as a fence, useful for building a clean seed of the right viewers rather than the cheapest ones (inference from Loomer's list).

Which interests still exist is only partly verifiable without a live ad account. Meta's browsable "Fitness and wellness" category still lists seven top-level interests (Bodybuilding, Meditation, Physical exercise, Physical fitness, Running, Weight training, Yoga) with everything else, including CrossFit, reachable only by keyword search (practitioner, [Audience Eye](https://audienceeye.com/facebook-ads-interests-list/); [Eachspy](https://www.eachspy.com/facebook-ads-interests/)). A 2026 interest-stacking guide uses "CrossFit", "HYROX", "Nike Training Club" and "Powerlifting" as selectable interests (practitioner, [Adamigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/how-to-build-interest-based-audiences-for-meta-ads)), and "Garmin Fitness" appears as an interest of about 5.3M (practitioner, undated, [AdTargeting](https://adtargeting.io/blog/target-fitness-audience-on-facebook-ad)). No source confirms "WHOOP", "Oura Ring", "Fitbit", "Apple Watch", "Polar", "Amazfit", "Biohacking", "Quantified self", "Sleep" or "Heart rate monitor" as current interests, and Meta consolidated many sport, food and music interests into broader groupings from June 23, 2025, after removing detailed-targeting exclusions on March 31, 2025, citing a 22.6% lower median cost per conversion without exclusions (practitioner summarising Meta notices, [FanIQ](https://www.faniq.live/blog/meta-targeting-update)). The authoritative check is Meta's Marketing API `adinterest` search, which needs a connected ad account ([KlientBoost](https://www.klientboost.com/facebook/facebook-interest-targeting/)).

What Meta removed as "sensitive" was health conditions and causes, not fitness activity. The January 2022 wave removed options "referencing causes, organizations, or public figures that relate to health, race or ethnicity, political affiliation, religion, or sexual orientation" (Meta-official, [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/removing-certain-ad-targeting-options-and-expanding-our-ad-controls)), including items such as "Continuous positive airway pressure" and "Cancer awareness" (journalism dataset, [The Markup](https://github.com/the-markup/facebook-removed-interests/blob/main/data/fb_removed_interests.csv)); a January 2024 wave removed or consolidated further unpublished options "that relate to topics people may perceive as sensitive" (trade press quoting Meta, [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/metas-removing-detailed-targeting-options-ad-campaigns/704375/)). Since **September 2, 2025**, "any custom audience or lookalike audience suggesting specific health conditions (e.g., 'arthritis', 'diabetes')… will be flagged and prevented from being used" (Meta-official, [Marketing API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-audience/)), and the same applies to custom conversions ([LiveRamp notice](https://docs.liveramp.com/connect/en/announcement--upcoming-meta-restrictions-on-certain-custom-audiences-and-custom-conversions--7-17-25-.html)). Meta's Health & Wellness ad standard additionally requires 18+ targeting for health products, exempts "fitness services and health clubs" and general wellbeing products, bans body-shaming and pinched-fat visuals, and specifically prohibits ads that "claim that results can be achieved solely by use of wearable products" (Meta-official, [Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/); [Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939)).

Audience-building mechanics are unchanged and need no pixel. Instagram business-profile engagement audiences (everyone who engaged, profile visitors, people who messaged, people who saved a post or ad) retain up to 730 days per the API, video audiences can be cut at 3s, ThruPlay, 25/50/75/95%, and lead-form audiences retain 90 days (Meta-official, [Engagement Custom Audiences](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/guides/engagement-custom-audiences/)). Lookalike sources "must have at least 100 members" from one country, with 1,000–5,000 recommended and sizes of 1–10% in Ads Manager (Meta-official, [Lookalike Audiences](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/guides/lookalike-audiences/); [Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/164749007013531)). Ad sets exit learning "after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit" (Meta-official, [Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)).

The evidence on broad versus interests is lopsided but has a small-budget caveat. Meta's WHOOP success story reports **29% lower cost per purchase and 30% higher ROAS** from broad targeting versus segmented audiences, with WHOOP saying it "implemented a broad audience strategy across all currently active regions" (Meta-official case study, undated, [Meta/Instagram success story](https://www.facebook.com/business/success/instagram/whoop)); a second researcher in this project could not open that page, so the figures should be read live before being quoted anywhere. Oura's VP Growth likewise says "we don't limit our audience targeting to any particular group" (company-reported via teardown, [The Digital Chapter](https://www.thedigitalchapter.com/p/oura-ring-growthmarketing-casestudy)). Against this, Berkay Barboros's 23 A/B tests found broad won 17 of 23 on CPA overall but "below €3,000/month spend, broad targeting underperformed in 15 of 17 tests," and he recommends "3–5 interest segments in a single ad set" for €0–3K/month accounts (practitioner test, [Berkay Barboros](https://berkaybarboros.com/blog/meta-ads-broad-targeting)). Loomer concedes suggestions "MAYBE… useful for new businesses or new accounts where Meta doesn't have data" (practitioner, [Jon Loomer Pubcast](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/how-i-actually-approach-targeting-in-2026/)). Both positions fit Vybe: interest suggestions cost nothing and can be seeded, but they cannot be relied on to fence delivery, and the WHOOP result came from a mature purchase-optimised account rather than a pre-launch one.

Andromeda is why creative carries the targeting load. Meta's engineering blog describes Andromeda (December 2024) as its "retrieval" system, the first stage that narrows tens of millions of ads to a few thousand per person, with a 10,000x increase in model complexity, +6% recall and +8% ad quality (Meta-official, [Engineering at Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/); [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/ai-innovation-in-metas-ads-ranking-driving-advertiser-performance)). Practitioners converge on the consequence: "Andromeda reads every element of your creative… as signals about who might respond," near-duplicate variants are clustered, and "five structurally different ad concepts typically outperform thirty micro-variants" (practitioner, [Foxwell Digital](https://www.foxwelldigital.com/blog/how-andromeda-has-changed-meta-advertising-a-practical-guide); [eChai summary](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-meta-ads-fundamentals/targeting-vs-broad-advantage-plus)). Vendor claims about a "60% similarity threshold" or "65% higher ROAS" from 20+ ads a month have no disclosed dataset and are unverified ([Chatterbuzz](https://www.chatterbuzzmedia.com/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-targeting/)).

## Health & wellness restrictions spare a clean pre-launch funnel

From January 2025 Meta assigns data-source categories to pixels and datasets, and a "Health & Wellness" flag brings one of three tiers: Core setup (custom parameters and URL paths after the domain are stripped), restriction of mid- and lower-funnel standard events (Purchase, AddToCart and, inconsistently, Lead), or full restriction of all events, under which "Meta Business Tools cannot be used for campaign optimization" (Meta-official wording via [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-restrictions-health-wellness-campaigns-453094); [Meta Business Help Centre](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/1666235380728268)). Meta's definition ties the category to data "associated with medical conditions, specific health statuses, or provider/patient relationships (for example, a patient portal or wellness tracker for depression)" (Meta wording via [Foxwell Digital](https://www.foxwelldigital.com/blog/meta-special-ad-category-vs-sensitive-ad-category-whats-the-difference)), so a wellness tracker sits inside the risk zone but a general-fitness band is less exposed than a condition-specific one; Foley Hoag's reading is that "products intended to support general wellness… may be less likely to fall within the health and wellness category" (law-firm analysis, [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/security-privacy-and-the-law/2025/january/meta-s-new-advertising-rules-key-considerations-for-health-and-wellness-businesses/)). No source documents WHOOP, Oura or Garmin being restricted, and WHOOP was running 282 active ads with SHOP_NOW CTAs in March 2026 (practitioner, [GoMarble](https://www.gomarble.ai/library/brands/whoop/)). Appeals are automated, "the domain will be the source of truth," and no successful overturn of a full restriction had been reported by July 2026 (practitioner, [Freshpaint](https://www.freshpaint.io/blog/meta-data-restrictions); [Matchnode](https://matchnode.com/metas-new-data-restrictions/)).

For Vybe the restriction is close to moot through May 2027, because everything a pre-launch account would optimise to survives Tier 2: upper-funnel standard events such as PageView, ViewContent and landing-page views remain, "In-app lead generation advertisers will not be impacted (beyond Core Setup)" and "ToFu/brand campaigns are not affected" (practitioner, [XBorder/SEL](https://xborderinsights.com/how-to-prevent-meta-ads-restrictions-on-health-and-wellness-campaigns/)). What matters is building the stack so a later flag never forces a rebuild: register custom events in Events Manager under neutral names (Freshpaint's example is "event_T4B9"), keep condition words out of URL paths, UTMs, button labels and audience names, and remember that CAPI does not bypass detection (practitioner, [Freshpaint](https://www.freshpaint.io/blog/meta-data-restrictions); [CustomerLabs](https://www.customerlabs.com/blog/meta-data-sharing-restrictions-what-it-means-for-health-brands/)). Because classification happens at the domain and dataset level, a separate pre-launch landing domain with no condition copy is a defensible hedge (inference from Meta's "domain is the source of truth" appeals rule). The same discipline serves the FDA and FTC boundaries identified in the earlier growth plan: wellness language ("trends", "insights") rather than "detects" or "medical-grade", "tend to coincide" rather than causal claims for the Connect pillar, and a privacy policy that matches "never sold" word for word ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/health-claims)).

One creative caution follows. Oura runs explicit cycle and fertility creative on Meta, including a Swedish Natural Cycles ad that ran 364 days and new video reading "Oura uses temperature trends to unlock more accurate insights about your menstrual cycle" (ad-library data via [AdWhispr](https://adwhispr.com), verifiable at the [Oura Ad Library page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=all&ad_type=all&country=US&view_all_page_id=753245374771837)), which shows such ad copy passes review. The risk is not the ad but the data trail behind it: Meta's Business Tools terms prohibit sharing "sexual and reproductive health" information (Meta-official via [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-restrictions-health-wellness-campaigns-453094)), so any women's-health persona ad should land on a generic page and feed a neutrally named audience.

## Competitors run static offer treadmills and leave the ownership buyer unaddressed

Ad-library data for September 30, 2026 shows WHOOP with 786 tracked ads (210 active), a hook mix of **589 direct-offer, 91 contrarian and 60 problem-agitation** ads, and a format mix of 425 text-overlay and 339 studio-product statics against only 11 video and 8 UGC ads; its longest-running active creative is a 92-day New Year evergreen ("Sleep deeper, train smarter, and build healthier habits this January") (ad-library data via [AdWhispr](https://adwhispr.com), [WHOOP Ad Library page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=all&ad_type=all&country=US&view_all_page_id=314298918730028)). GoMarble's independent count puts WHOOP at 282 active ads, 68% image, "93% of WHOOP ads use SHOP_NOW CTA" (practitioner, snippet-level, [GoMarble](https://www.gomarble.ai/library/brands/whoop/)). WHOOP's women's-hormonal-insights and longevity creative exists but has been cycled off. Oura's 554 tracked ads (67 active) are even more product-centric, 489 studio-product and 352 direct-offer, with a 20-day longest-running active ad, but Oura layers creator whitelisting ("#OuraPartner" posts running 123–384 days, heavily in DACH) and is the only incumbent running women's-health creative ([Oura Ad Library page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=all&ad_type=all&country=US&view_all_page_id=753245374771837)). Oura's disclosed Meta edge is data rather than targeting: Target/Roundel retail signals feeding "automatic AI driven optimization of media delivery in the Meta environment," with ROAS up about 30% and Roundel spend tripled (company-reported via trade press snippet, [Chief Marketer](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/ces-2026-oura-triples-investment-in-targets-retail-media-network/)).

Garmin outspends both, with an estimated $2.3–4.2M across 289 active ads and evergreen spec statics running 440 days, plus recovery-themed copy ("Your recovery matters… track your HRV while you sleep") (ad-library data via [AdWhispr](https://adwhispr.com), [Garmin Ad Library page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=all&ad_type=all&country=US&view_all_page_id=8329645705)). Amazfit is the most instructive competitor for Vybe: all 25 classified ads are studio-product, its recurring authority hook is "Trusted By HYROX Athletes… the official wearable technology and timing partner of HYROX," and a semantic search of its tracked ads for the Helio Strap returned only smartwatch creative ([Amazfit Ad Library page](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=all&ad_type=all&country=US&view_all_page_id=414662412400702)). Combined with Zepp's under-supply admission, the $99 no-subscription band is essentially unadvertised on US Meta. Polar Loop ("Pay once. Own it," no data selling, [Polar press release](https://www.polar.com/en/media-room/polar-launches-polar-loop-first-screen-free-subscription-free-wearable)), Hume Band 2.0 ($249, core free, [Geeky Gadgets](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/hume-band-2-review-2026/)), Luna Band ($149, [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/wearables/luna-is-betting-youre-tired-of-fitness-subscriptions-and-offering-its-screenless-band-as-the-solution/)) and Ultrahuman (~800,000 rings, $140M annualised revenue, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/qualcomm-backs-ultrahuman-in-70m-round-on-bet-to-turn-smart-rings-into-computers/)) have no measurable tracked Meta footprint; their "no subscription" positioning is winning in reviews and on Reddit, where buyers say "I don't want another subscription so Whoop is out" but also that cheaper bands are "not as useful as the whoop for actionable data" (Reddit aggregate, [RedditRecs](https://redditrecs.com/fitness-tracker/model/polar-loop/)). The gap for Vybe is therefore not a demographic but a message and a proof. Five audiences appear in no tracked creative: the explicit "no subscription, own your data" value shopper; men 45+ interested in recovery and longevity (Oura's "spry seniors" campaign was TV/OOH-led, [Campaign US](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/creative-fix-ouras-spry-seniors-give-us-finger/1919681)); beginners intimidated by "Strain" language; high-stress non-athletes; and privacy buyers. The recurring objection to challengers is software depth, so creative that shows a concrete daily recommendation rather than a score would pre-empt the comment-section attack. Because at least six rivals now claim "no subscription" and "screenless" (see the earlier growth plan), Vybe's "own and create" message has to be carried by what is structurally hard to copy: "never sold," "no Premium tier, ever," the Connect pillar, and the open SDK shown as "what people built on Vybe."

## A sub-$5K reference structure: two campaigns, broad ad sets, personas in creative

Nothing in this section is a recommendation to spend; it is the structure the evidence supports if the founder chooses to. The arithmetic constraint is Meta's 50-results-per-week learning rule (Meta-official, [Meta Business Help](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)). At $20–50 a day, only cheap, high-frequency events clear it. ThruPlays have been bought at under $0.01 each in a comparable pre-availability medtech campaign (agency-claimed, [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/pulsenmore-paid-social-awareness-case-study/)), while Meta lead campaigns run about **$6.00 per lead** across Metricool's 44,355-advertiser SMB sample and $27.66 in WordStream's 2025 benchmark (aggregators, [Netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/meta-ad-roas-fell-nearly-1-usd-per-dollar-spent-in-2026-report-finds/); [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025)), so a $10–15/day lead ad set yields 2–15 leads a week and should be judged on CPL trend rather than learning status. Practitioners agree on consolidation: "If your total monthly budget is $5,000, run two to three ad sets maximum rather than six to eight," and scale "no more than 20–30% every 3–5 days" (practitioner, [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/facebook-ads-minimum-budget-requirements)); Foxwell runs "one ad set with up to 20–30 creatives" and waits "at least 5–7 days before making any decisions" (practitioner, [Foxwell Digital](https://www.foxwelldigital.com/blog/how-andromeda-has-changed-meta-advertising-a-practical-guide)).

There is still no "optimise for Instagram follows" goal. The closest lever is a Traffic or Engagement campaign with `destination_type=INSTAGRAM_PROFILE` and optimisation goal `PROFILE_VISIT` or `PROFILE_AND_PAGE_ENGAGEMENT`, which runs only on Meta-owned placements and carries a "Visit Instagram profile" button (Meta-official, [Marketing API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/ad-creative/profile-visit-ads/instagram-profile-visit)). Since July 7, 2025 an "Instagram follows" column attributes follows to any campaign at ad level (Meta rollout via trade press, [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-introduces-instagram-follows-metric-for-ads-reporting/)), which lets Vybe compute follow rate and cost per follow per creative. Loomer warns that follow-style optimisation "has historically yielded only very low-quality" results and calls follows "a vanity metric" (practitioner, [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/instagram-follows/)); a small US hardware founder paid about $1 per follow and judged them low-intent (brand-reported via vendor, [Magnet](https://www.marketingbymagnet.com/case-studies/seatmate-chairs)). Because profile visits are a cheap action Meta "will try to find people who click easily" (practitioner, [JobDoor](https://www.jobdoor.co.uk/how-to-get-instagram-followers-with-meta-ads/)), this is the one campaign where narrower fitness suggestions or a video-viewer seed as the source audience is defensible. Partnership (whitelisted creator) ads support the Instagram-profile destination (Meta-official, [Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/1173889323625775/)) and Meta's 2,400-advertiser test found them 19% cheaper per result (Meta-official, [Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/metaforbusiness/posts/your-shoppers-already-trust-creators-add-their-content-with-partnership-ads-to-h/1586625773505122/)), though an independent $130M dataset found +19% CPM alongside +19% CTR (agency dataset, [Netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/meta-partnership-ads-outperform-licensed-ugc-across-130m-usd-in-ad-spend-report-finds/)).

The founder does not want "join the waitlist" to be the message, and the evidence supports alternatives: a lead magnet or quiz as the capture (Sourhouse pushes "many digital ads to the lead magnet instead of trying to make a direct sale," brand-reported, [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/built-following-before-product/)), comment-to-DM delivery that simultaneously builds the "messaged" custom audience (agency-claimed, [Contra](https://contra.com/p/3ZPbA7ke-how-stitch-space-generated-1000-launch-users)), and a late "founding member" $1 or refundable reserve for the warm pool only, where LaunchBoom claims a $1-deposit lead is "30 times more likely to buy" and Seatmate saw 26% waitlist-to-backer (agency-claimed and brand-reported, [LaunchBoom](https://www.launchboom.com/crowdfunding-guides/how-to-launch-a-successful-kickstarter-campaign/); [Magnet](https://www.marketingbymagnet.com/case-studies/seatmate-chairs)). Lead-form audiences expire at 90 days, so leads must be exported to a customer list to survive the eight-month runway (Meta-official retention windows, [Engagement Custom Audiences](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/guides/engagement-custom-audiences/)).

Measurement without purchase events rests on metrics Meta reports natively plus one discipline. Native: Instagram follows and profile visits per ad (and their ratio), ThruPlay and 3-second view rates, saves and messages (both retargetable, Meta-official, [Engagement Custom Audiences](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/guides/engagement-custom-audiences/)), cost per instant-form lead, frequency and new-reach share. On-site: landing-page views and neutrally named registered custom events, with the dataset self-categorised in Events Manager (Meta-official, [Meta Business Help Centre](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/1666235380728268)). Attribution: Nutrisense's rebuild moved to 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view, dropping 1-day view (agency-claimed, [Taikun](https://taikundigital.com/case-studies/full-funnel-meta-rebuild-for-a-health-and-wellness-brand)). Because last-click on a $30/day account is noise, a simple holdout (excluding one state for two weeks and comparing organic follow and lead velocity) is the cheapest incrementality read; geo-lift is how Lumens measured a 4–6% lift after launching Meta with no history, though at a far larger budget (agency-claimed, [Block & Tam](https://www.blockandtam.com/lumens)). Planning ranges for costs are proxies only: consumer-electronics CPM around $12.88 (Varos, April 2025, [Varos](https://www.varos.com/benchmarks/facebook-cpm-for-consumer-electronics)), fitness CTR 2.24% (aggregator, [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)), electronics CPA $51.86 with a 14.3-day consideration window (aggregator snippet, [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)), and no credible cost-per-follow or cost-per-lead benchmark for wearables exists.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "How should targeting be built" turns out to mean "how should creative and audiences be built," because Meta has withdrawn nearly every lever that once let an advertiser fence a persona, while leaving intact the tools that turn engagement into retargetable, lookalike-able assets without a pixel. For a brand with 30 followers and no history, that is an advantage: the same Reels the earlier growth plan prescribes for organic reach are the exact inputs Andromeda needs, and the saves, messages and 75%-plus views they generate are the audiences a launch campaign would retarget in May 2027. The distinctive finding on the competitive side is that the category's loudest press message, "no subscription, own it," is entirely unpurchased on Meta, and that the only recurring objection to it is software depth. Whoever first pairs the ownership message with visible proof of a daily recommendation holds open inventory.

Two implications follow for the founder's decision. First, the sequencing risk is asymmetric: the audiences (engagers, savers, messagers, leads) retain for 365–730 days, so anything built from October compounds into launch, whereas a spring start would leave lookalike seeds under the 1,000-person threshold when they are needed. Second, the constraints that protect Vybe from Meta's health restrictions, the FDA's wellness boundary, the FTC's claims rules and HYROX's brand terms all point the same way, toward performance-and-ownership framing on a clean domain with neutral naming. The targeting plan and the compliance plan are, in practice, the same document.
