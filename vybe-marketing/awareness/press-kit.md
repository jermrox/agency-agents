## 1. Three key messages

Each is one sentence. Each stands without a health claim. "Designed to" governs every sentence that rests on an unshipped design commitment (rows 14, 15, 18).

1. **Vybe is designed to answer in a plain sentence that says how sure it is and what it could not see, instead of handing you a score.** (rows 14 and 15; the one idea no competitor claims, per `concept.md`.)
2. **Vybe is a band and a platform: five connected signals read against your own changing baseline, designed so other teams can build on it.** (rows 3, 9 and 18; never "available now", never "first" or "only".)
3. **The band has no required subscription, and your health data is never sold; shared only with your consent, with service providers that run the service, or where the law requires.** (rows 2 and 4, in the Register's exact wording; never "nothing shared".)

What these never become: a diagnosis, "clinical-grade", "validated", an accuracy figure, ECG or heart-rhythm wording, a battery number, a ship date, or a partner's name before it is signed.

## 5. Pitch rules the awareness agent follows

1. **One angle per pitch.** Never two ideas in one email. The angle is a story the journalist's readers care about; Vybe is the evidence, not the subject line.
2. **Under 200 words.** Subject line under eight words, angle first, company name last.
3. **Personalised first paragraph, every time.** Name the writer's piece and what in it connects; the agent has read their last ten pieces before drafting, not only the three listed here. A pitch with a visible template is spam.
4. **One ask.** A 15-minute call, a loaner band when units exist, or a data walk-through. Never two asks, never "let me know your thoughts".
5. **One follow-up.** Three to five working days later, two sentences, then stop. Never a third email, never a nudge through another channel.
6. **Never on a Monday or a Friday.** Tuesday to Thursday, morning in the journalist's time zone.
7. **Never two journalists on one email.** One email, one recipient. No CC, no BCC between outlets, and never the same exclusive offered twice.
8. **Never a release on a first pitch.** No attachment, no link to a release. The press line from section 3 is offered on request, after a reply.
9. **Written as John, sent only after a yes.** First person is John, signature is "John, Director of Growth, Vybe Health" with the Vybe calendar link under it; John is on CC; the founder has approved the outlet list first (rule 11). The agent drafts; the human sends.
10. **Rule 4 before every send.** Search the text for the two forbidden strings; if either appears, do not send.
11. **Rule 7 in every sentence.** A collaboration, an integration or an investment story. Never a booth, a table, a sponsor slot or a supplier. Never an individual's inbox when a role inbox exists.
12. **Claims as written in the Register.** "Designed to" on rows 14 to 17, 19 and 22; row 4 in its exact words; "HRV and heart rate", never ECG; no battery number; no "validated"; no partner named before written consent; no ship date.
13. **Off the record means off the record; embargoes are kept.** A journalist never learns anything from Vybe that turns out to be untrue.
14. **Measure honestly.** Pitches sent, replies, pieces run, tier of outlet; one reply is one reply, never "traction".
