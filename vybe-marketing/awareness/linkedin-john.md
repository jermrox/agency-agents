## 1. Voice profile: John, Director of Growth, Vybe Health

John runs growth for a wearable-health platform company in Akron, Ohio. He
writes like someone who has shipped things, not like someone who posts about
shipping things. He says what Vybe knows, what it does not know yet, and when
it will. He never announces. He invites.

**Tone in three words:** Plain. Specific. Honest.

**On-voice (write like this):**
1. "Most wearables hand you a score. We'd rather hand you a sentence that says how sure it is."
2. "We're pre-launch. Here is what we know, what we don't, and when we'll know."
3. "A good wearable idea shouldn't force a team to become a hardware company. That's the problem we picked."
4. "Twenty bands on one team people have heard of beats two thousand on a crowd that forgets us by Monday."
5. "HRV moved. That's an observation, not a verdict. Five things could explain it."

**Off-voice (never like this):**
1. "Excited to announce that we're disrupting the wearable space!"
2. "Your body is trying to tell you something. Are you listening?"
3. "Accuracy you can trust, backed by science." (an accuracy claim with no method behind it)
4. "We're the first and only open wearable platform."
5. "Drop a fire emoji if you agree!"

**Standing rules for every post in John's name:**
- First person is John. "We" is Vybe. "I" is John's own view or his own moment.
- One idea per paragraph. Two or three lines each. White space is the format.
- A defensible take in every post. State it, name the counterargument, hold the line.
- End on a question a real person can answer. Never "like if you agree".
- No links in the body. Links go in the first comment.
- Three to five specific hashtags. Never more.
- Tag nobody unless they are part of the story and have said yes.
- Health claims: observed, associated, personally supported, hypothesis, in that order of strength. Sleep stages are estimates. HRV is sensitive, not specific. No diagnosis, no "clinical-grade", no "validated", no ECG or heart-rhythm wording, no battery number.
- App promises (answer in a sentence, confidence in words, what it could not see, grading its own advice, patterns not causes) are "designed to" or "built to" on the sentence's main verb, every time, until the founder says they shipped.
- Example answers are quoted only as "prototype, sample data" and always name more than one explanation.
- Platform: "built open", "designed to be built on", "design partners", "talk to us". Never "available now", never "first" or "only", never that an SDK or raw signal access exists.
- Data: "never sold" may stand alone. If sharing comes up, the Claim Register wording in full: "shared only with your consent, with service providers that run the service, or where the law requires". Never "nothing shared".
- No number about Vybe (followers, users, waitlist, revenue, partners) unless the founders supply it. Where one is needed: [NUMBER: founders fill].
- The hardware is built. Vybe never says, or implies, that it needs engineering, design or manufacturing help.
- No partner, pilot or conversation is stated as happening until the founder confirms it and the partner has agreed in writing to be named.
- No booth, table, expo, sponsor slot or sampling stand, ever (rule 7). A big-name win is a collaboration, not an event presence (rule 12).
- Every draft is marked "DRAFT: needs John's yes" until he replies yes to it by number.

A score can't tell you how sure it is. That's the whole problem with wearables.

The big brands now show you how the score is calculated. Good. That was overdue.

But a score still can't do two things. It can't tell you how confident it is. And it can't tell you what it missed.

We're pre-launch, and we'll get some of these wrong. The app is designed to keep score on its own advice, misses included.

### Post 4. Pillar 3, data. "HRV is sensitive, not specific."

**DRAFT: needs John's yes**

Hook 1 (data lead):
"28,175 people. About nine million measurements. One finding: HRV is sensitive, not specific."

Hook 2 (bold claim):
"When an app tells you your HRV dropped because of the wine, it's guessing. Confidently."

Hook 3 (curiosity gap):
"The most useful number on your wrist is also the easiest one to misread."

Body:

28,175 people. About nine million measurements. One finding: HRV is sensitive, not specific.

That's Altini and Plews, 2021. HRV and resting heart rate move with training, alcohol, sickness and menstrual-cycle phase. All of them. Often at once.

So when an app tells you your HRV dropped because of the wine, it's guessing. Confidently.

That's why Vybe reads five factors as one system. Restore, Move, Nourish, Connect, Vitals. A change in one is read next to the others.

The app is designed to name the explanations that fit, in order, instead of one culprit.

Here's a Nourish example from our prototype. Sample data, not a real person:

"On nights you ate after 21:00, your HRV ran lower. Late dinners fit; so do the later bedtimes on those nights. Low confidence: six such nights. Next: try an earlier dinner twice this week."

A pattern is not a cause. The app is designed to say so, and to show the weeks a pattern broke at the same size as the weeks it held.

One more thing while we're being careful. Sleep stages on any wrist device are estimates. Lab polysomnography is the reference, and wrist trackers differ from it on total sleep time, efficiency and wake after sleep onset (Lee et al., 2025, 24 studies).

None of this is a verdict on your body. It's a better conversation with your own data.

What's the explanation your tracker gives you that you stopped believing?

## 6. The engagement routine

The first hour decides a post's reach. John's presence is the post. Nothing
here is a DM, a pitch or a spend; all three need a go-ahead first.

**Before publishing (the hour before):**
- Leave five to ten substantive comments on posts from the target kinds below. A substantive comment extends the idea or asks a real question. "Great post" is a wasted slot.
- State the affiliation when it matters: "I run growth at Vybe Health, so I'm biased, but..."
- Never comment to steer someone to a Vybe post. Never leave a link.

**The first 60 minutes after publishing:**
- Reply to every comment. Questions and genuine takes first, agreement second, emoji last.
- Each reply adds one thing: a source, a limit, a counterexample, a follow-up question. Never a bare "thanks".
- Pin nothing in the first hour; pin the best substantive comment at the end of it.
- A vendor, supplier or event organiser who comments gets a courteous one-line reply and no follow-up. It is a sales reply, not a lead (rule 7).
- A journalist who comments is noted for the awareness desk; John does not pitch in the thread.
- A question about whether someone has a condition gets the answer the app is designed to give: that is outside what Vybe does, and a clinician is the right person.
- Corrections are made in the thread, in plain words, the same hour. A health-claim correction after publishing is a defect, and the goal is zero.

**Daily, three to five target accounts (kinds, not people):**
1. Human-performance and sports-science staff at flagship universities and their labs.
2. Performance, athlete-health and research staff at national governing bodies and pro or college leagues.
3. Tactical and military human-performance programme leads (Holistic Health and Fitness, operator-readiness programmes).
4. Researchers who publish on HRV, sleep estimation and wearable validation.
5. Builders: digital-health founders, health-API commentators, developer-platform leads at health-data companies.
6. Consumer-health-data privacy lawyers and commentators.
7. Ohio and Midwest startup ecosystem accounts: regional funds, accelerators, university commercialisation offices.

Ranked by `big_name` first (rule 12), then fit. Comment before Vybe needs anything from them. Connection requests are personalised and reference a specific post; never the default text.

**Never without a go-ahead:** a DM, a boost, a paid audience, a journalist pitch, naming a partner, posting in John's name.
