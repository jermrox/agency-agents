## 1. What comparable companies had when they raised

We looked at 19 consumer sleep, stress and wellness wearables that raised pre-seed, seed or Series A money between 2022 and 2026. **Every one of them had at least two of these five things:**

1. **Their own science or patents.**
   - Elemind: 5 clinical trials and patents, $12M seed.
   - Somnee: a published sleep study and 24 patents, $10M (Khosla).
   - Lumia: peer-reviewed validation, 11 patents and $5.1M in government contracts.
2. **Prepaid demand.**
   - Oura: a Kickstarter of about $650K before its seed round.
   - Lief: $406K on Kickstarter.
   - Prophetic: raised $1.1M (led by BoxGroup) with **no working device**, on $100 refundable deposits plus a design partner.
3. **Revenue or units shipped.**
   - Sensate: about $10M run-rate (unverified).
   - Roga: 500+ units and 5 corporate clients before its C$1.7M pre-seed.
4. **A team with a strong track record.** Former Bose (Lumia), former Fitbit (Somnee), MIT/Harvard (Elemind).
5. **A distribution or credibility partner.** NBA teams (Somnee), Philips (NOWATCH), the US Air Force (Lumia).

**The honest gap.**
- Vybe has none of the five today.
- HRV, heart rate and sleep timing are measured by every competitor.
- "No subscription" and "plain language" are product choices, not something competitors can't copy.
- Garmin CIRQA and Polar Loop now also sell screen-free bands with no subscription.

## 2. The three routes that fit Vybe's situation (no money, no travel)

1. **Refundable deposits.** This is the cheapest of the five to build, and it is what investors actually paid for (Prophetic, Oura, NOWATCH). It needs:
   - an honest deposit page: one sentence a day, no subscription, data never sold;
   - a target number of deposits, set before launch.
2. **A small accuracy study.** About 30–50 people, comparing Vybe's readings against a reference device, ideally with NC State, Duke or UNC. Earable, Clair and Lumia all raised on small studies like this. It must stay within general-wellness claims.
3. **Strategic angels from the wearable world.** Their money comes with credibility and introductions (see section 4).

## 5. How to get warm introductions online, without paying

- **Through founders the investor already backed.** Founders of non-competing portfolio companies (for example Sahha, Arcascope or Lumia) are the most credible route to their investors. Ask one for advice before asking for an introduction.
- **Through public contribution.** Thoughtful, data-backed posts where these people already are, for example Altini's HRV4Training community and Supermoon's research. Always say you are the founder.
- **Through local operators.** In Raleigh, Steven LeBoeuf.
- **Through published routes.** Use a fund's own form or pitch inbox only where it publishes one.

## 6. Before any pitch

- **Legal setup.** Choose a securities route with counsel before saying "raising" anywhere in public. Rule 506(b) allows no public promotion. Rule 506(c) allows promotion, but only to verified accredited investors.
- **Answer "why not a Garmin?"** in one page.
- **Have at least one of the five assets in section 1 underway.** Deposits are the fastest.

## 7. Strategic routes: who would gain from Vybe existing

**What matters most: hardware is not the moat.**
- Companies that hand out bands in bulk buy cheap bands from contract manufacturers and put their own brand on them. Personify Health launched its own "Max GO wearable device" for employers (its press release, confirmed).
- Screenless HRV bands with an open SDK are sold white-label:
  - J-Style's guide says first orders are typically 500–1,000 units, delivered in 4–8 weeks.
  - Alibaba listings start at about $17–19 per unit.
- Vybe's value is the daily plain-language insight and the data promise, not the circuit board.

**Realistic plays, in order:**
1. **Write Vybe's data to Apple Health and Google Health Connect from day one.**
   - Employer and team platforms already take data from both. For example, Teamworks AMS moves "Garmin, Polar, Oura, and Apple HealthKit" data.
   - So Vybe can say "works with your wellness programme" with no deal and no security audit.
   - The developer accounts cost $99 a year (Apple) and $25 once (Google).
2. **Run a small validation group in Raleigh.** 30–60 people for 8 weeks. Measure:
   - how many keep wearing it;
   - how often they read the daily sentence;
   - how closely it agrees with a reference device.

   Every buyer and investor here asks for this.
3. **Fund the first production order with refundable pre-orders.** Revenue-based financing such as Wayflyer requires "at least USD 10,000 average monthly revenue for at least the previous 6 months". Kickfurther requires "$400K+" trailing revenue. Pre-orders are the only option that works before revenue.
4. **After launch: apply as a Wellhub app partner.** Wellhub has a self-serve partner process and is the one employer-wellness channel a small company can enter online.
5. **With data: pitch Supermoon Capital.** ResMed is an investor in Supermoon's sleep fund (secondary source). Supermoon is the most aligned investor found.

**The deal shape to aim for later.** Tempur Sealy made "a $25 million equity investment in Fullpower to acquire an approximate 15.6% ownership stake", alongside a licence for Fullpower's sleep software (Somnigroup's own release, confirmed). That is what licensing an insight layer looks like once it is proven at scale.

**Not realistic at this stage:**
- Insurers subsidising Vybe. John Hancock Vitality and Discovery Vitality fund Apple Watch, Oura, WHOOP and Garmin only.
- Personify buying Vybe bands; it has its own.
- A corporate venture arm leading a pre-launch round.
- Inventory or revenue-based financing before revenue.
- Building custom hardware before 500 white-label units have sold.
