#### Q39. What makes your solution innovative? (Limit 300 words)

Solve's guidance: how the approach is new or significantly improved; how it is distinct from others using a similar technology; how it could catalyse broader impact; how it could change the market.

Everyone else stops at the score. The innovation is the step after it, and three choices make that step possible.

First, context. A reading can move for reasons no sensor can see: a flight, a hard week, a heat wave, a late dinner. Vybe reads the body's signals alongside that context, from the person's calendar, the weather and their own notes, and says which of them the change is most likely about. Other products cannot do this because they do not ask.

Second, the baseline is the person, never a population. Every signal is read against the wearer's own recent range. A resting heart rate of 60 is high for one person and low for the next. Vybe only ever says where today sits in your own band.

Third, the loop closes. Vybe suggests one thing, not six, and then reports whether it worked, including the suggestions that missed. The outcome record is the product's claim to be trusted, and it is the part competitors do not ship.

Underneath those is the model that makes them reachable: no required subscription. The interpretation comes with the Band. That inverts the category's economics, in which the intelligence is the monthly fee, and it is the reason the product can serve a fixed income at all.

A licensed SDK lets other builders put the same overnight signals inside their own products, so the approach can spread without every team rebuilding the hardware.
