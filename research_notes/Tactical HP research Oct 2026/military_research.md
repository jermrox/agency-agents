# Military Human Performance Research Findings (2025-2026)

## USARIEM Research: Physical Performance, Load Carriage, Heat/Cold, Altitude, Nutrition

### Takeaway
USARIEM continues to lead DoD research on environmental extremes, with 2025-2026 publications advancing thermophysiological modeling for heat injury prediction, cold-weather load carriage physiology, and female warfighter performance in extreme environments.

### Cited Findings
- USARIEM assessed heat injury risk for the 2026 Bataan Memorial Death March using thermophysiological modeling, finding that fast-paced participants can exceed critical core temperature thresholds (>39C) within approximately one hour, with metabolic workload strongly influencing heat strain trajectories independent of environmental conditions. Authors include Potter, Giersch, Xu, and Castellani. — [SSRN Preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6506483) (Grade: C - modeling study/preprint)
- A 2026 USARIEM study published in Wilderness & Environmental Medicine found that immediate load carriage improves core temperature but not skin temperatures when wearing wet clothing in mild cold air, with implications for cold-weather operational planning. Authors: Bodurtha, Shaw, Greenfield, Pitsas, Alba, Castellani, O'Brien, Seeley. — [Wilderness & Environmental Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032251376323) (Grade: C - small experimental study)
- A 2025 PMC article addressed "Optimizing Female Warfighter Health and Performance in Environmental Extremes," providing targeted research on sex-specific physiological responses to heat, cold, and altitude in military populations. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12186821/) (Grade: B - narrative review with military population focus)
- USARIEM researchers are actively hunting for biological indicators (biomarkers) of heat stress risk, recruiting 24 volunteer Soldiers at Fort Rucker who provided blood and urine samples and wore instrumentation for monitoring heart rate, skin temperature, and core temperature during a five-mile run and eight-mile ruck march. — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/288109/usariem_researchers_hunt_for_biological_indicators_of_heat_stress_risk) (Grade: C - pilot/ongoing)
- A 2024 PMC article on energy expenditure during physical work in cold environments provides updated physiology and performance considerations for military service members. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11486477/) (Grade: B - review)
- A 2025 PMC review on preparation for heat season training outlined key considerations for heat injury risk prevention and mitigation in military populations. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12360643/) (Grade: B - review with military focus)

### Inferences
- USARIEM's thermophysiological modeling capability is maturing to the point of event-specific predictive risk assessments, which could be operationalized for unit-level heat injury prevention planning.
- The emphasis on female-specific environmental physiology research signals growing institutional recognition of sex-based differences in thermal regulation and altitude acclimatization.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 USARIEM publications found specifically on altitude/high-terrestrial performance optimization (their Thermal and Mountain Medicine Division lists this as a focus area, but recent publications were not surfaced).
- Specific USARIEM nutrition research publications for 2025-2026 were not directly captured; their nutrition work may be embedded in broader CHAMP/HPRC outputs.

## OPSS: Supplement Use and Safety in Military Populations

### Takeaway
OPSS remains the DoD's mandated dietary supplement safety program, maintaining a prohibited ingredients list of approximately 800 items and providing screening tools, with ongoing concerns about undeclared ingredients in supplements marketed to military populations.

### Cited Findings
- DoD Instruction 6130.06 formalizes OPSS as the Department of Defense program for dietary supplements, mandating dietary supplement education and condition of use throughout the military community since 2022. — [Health.mil](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2025/07/29/Service-Members-and-Dietary-Supplements-What-You-Need-to-Know)
- The OPSS DoD Prohibited Dietary Supplement Ingredient List currently contains approximately 800 items with some 4,600 synonyms, updating quarterly based on emerging scientific evidence and FDA regulatory action. — [Military.com](https://www.military.com/feature/2026/01/14/dietary-supplements-prohibited-ingredients-and-militarys-ongoing-enforcement-challenge.html)
- Supplements with the highest adverse effect reports are those marketed for weight loss, pre/post-workout use, and prohormones. The presence of undeclared ingredients in some dietary supplements creates both health risks and compliance risks for military personnel subject to strict drug-use rules. — [Military.com](https://www.military.com/feature/2026/01/14/dietary-supplements-prohibited-ingredients-and-militarys-ongoing-enforcement-challenge.html)
- Third-party certification programs (NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport) are increasingly emphasized as safeguards for military supplement users. — [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/02/25/the-future-of-performance-supplementation-is-certified-and-informed/)

### Inferences
- The quarterly update cycle for the prohibited list indicates OPSS is actively responsive to emerging supplement risks.
- Third-party certification is becoming the de facto standard recommendation for military supplement use.

### Gaps
- No specific 2025-2026 OPSS research publications or adverse event summaries were found; OPSS functions primarily as an education and policy program rather than a research entity.

## PubMed: Military Fitness, Tactical Athlete Performance, Musculoskeletal Injury Prevention

### Takeaway
Recent peer-reviewed literature demonstrates significant advances in understanding musculoskeletal injury risk factors in basic combat training, the economic value of H2F performance teams, and the role of holistic health approaches in injury prevention.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 systematic review with meta-analysis in Military Medicine (Vol. 190, Issue 5-6) found that individuals with prior musculoskeletal injuries exhibited significant reduction in isokinetic peak muscle force, notably affecting knee and shoulder regions (meta-analysis of 1,267 participants). — [Military Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1093/milmed/usae499) (Grade: A - systematic review/meta-analysis)
- A June 2025 JAMA Network Open study of 2,988 Army basic combat trainees identified 27 modifiable and nonmodifiable risk factors for musculoskeletal injury during the 10-week BCT period, supporting tiered quantification screening and multifactorial interventions prior to intense physical training. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12131099) (Grade: A - large prospective cohort)
- A 2026 Journal of Veterans Studies article examined musculoskeletal injury prevention across military and veteran populations, focusing on the role of sleep, stress, and holistic health. — [Journal of Veterans Studies](https://journal-veterans-studies.org/articles/10.21061/jvs.v12i1.879) (Grade: B - cross-domain review)
- A July 2026 Military Medicine article titled "Enhancing Military Musculoskeletal Readiness: A Proposal for Collaboration" advocated for coordinated approaches to MSKI prevention. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41734198/) (Grade: B - commentary/proposal)
- A July 2026 Sports Medicine article evaluated the ROI of H2F Performance Teams using a matched difference-in-differences design across 56 brigades and more than one million Soldiers (FY2019-FY2023). Every $1 invested in HPTs returns $8.15 in value ($4.69 in cost avoidances, $3.46 in readiness improvements). Mean annual cost avoidance was $14.06M per brigade (95% CI $12.25-16.19M), with 99.05% of Monte Carlo draws exceeding a 4:1 ROI. Force-wide extrapolation yields $5.28 billion in annual Army savings. — [Sports Medicine / PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41678032/) (Grade: A - large quasi-experimental study)
- A July 2026 Military Medicine article on "Human Performance Optimization in Special Operations Forces: Structure, Outcomes, and Readiness Implications" searched databases from January 2010 to December 2025 to review SOF HPO program structures and outcomes. — [Military Medicine / PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42391107/) (Grade: A - systematic review)

### Inferences
- The H2F ROI study provides the strongest economic evidence to date supporting investment in embedded performance teams, with implications for resourcing decisions across all services.
- The BCT injury risk factor study's identification of 27 factors supports increasingly personalized, data-driven screening approaches.

### Gaps
- Limited 2025-2026 RCTs specifically on tactical athlete performance interventions were found; most evidence remains observational or quasi-experimental.

## Sleep Research

### Takeaway
Military sleep research in 2025-2026 focuses on the TBI-sleep connection (glymphatic dysfunction), CBTI implementation in military TBI clinics, and the downstream effects of sleep disruption on readiness and cognitive function.

### Cited Findings
- MIT-developed wearable headset using near-infrared light and EEG sensors detected that individuals with mild TBI may have disrupted glymphatic function during deep sleep, a critical phase for neurological waste clearance. — [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2025/new-technologies-tackle-brain-health-assessment-for-military-0825) (Grade: C - pilot technology validation)
- A VA Million Veteran Program study assessed effects of TBI history and sleep on subjective cognition in Veterans. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41273924/) (Grade: B - large cohort)
- A 2026 quality improvement project assessed integration of a smartphone application with behavioral treatment (CBTI) in a military TBI clinic. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12713873/) (Grade: C - QI project)
- Exposure to brighter light after waking is associated with better sleep efficiency among veterans with insomnia and TBI history. — [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2025/new-technologies-tackle-brain-health-assessment-for-military-0825) (Grade: B)

### Inferences
- Glymphatic function monitoring could become a biomarker-based approach to assessing brain health and recovery in service members with TBI history.
- The gap between CBTI evidence and clinical implementation in military settings represents a translational research priority.

### Gaps
- No large-scale RCTs on military sleep optimization interventions (beyond TBI populations) were found for 2025-2026.

## Wearable Technology and Performance Monitoring

### Takeaway
The LifeLens Wearable Platform began fielding to SOF in late 2025, tracking 400+ metrics with zero field failures in initial testing, while the Army Sustainment Command expanded smartwatch/ring programs for general force fitness monitoring.

### Cited Findings
- The LifeLens Wearable Platform developed by LifeLens Technologies Inc. began fielding to special operations community elements in late 2025, with broader FY2026 delivery planned. The platform delivers real-time, clinical-grade vitals (heart rate, respiration rate, core temperature) from warfighter to command post and tracks 400+ metrics covering performance, fitness, recovery, sleep, and injury risk.
