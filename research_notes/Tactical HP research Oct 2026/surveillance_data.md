# Military Health Surveillance Data (2025-2026)

## What are the latest MSMR findings on musculoskeletal injury, TBI, heat illness, and mental health conditions in service members?

### Takeaway
The 2025 MSMR data shows musculoskeletal conditions remain the dominant driver of ambulatory healthcare burden (70.9% rate increase in visits from 2020-2024), mental health disorder incidence remains persistently high at 10,990.6 per 100,000 person-years (2025), heat stroke incidence rose 6.9% year-over-year in 2025, and in-theater musculoskeletal encounters grew from 21.1% to 31.4% of all encounters (2021-2025).

### Cited Findings
- Musculoskeletal system conditions accounted for the highest growth in ambulatory visits, totaling an additional 1,675,234 visits (rate increase of 70.9%) from 2020 to 2024, followed by mental health disorders (650,888 more visits, 53.2% rate increase) — [MSMR Ambulatory Health Care Visits, 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12530864/)
- The sustained high incidence of mental health disorders (10,990.6 per 100,000 person-years) in 2025 and high rates of diagnostic comorbidity among active component service members directly compromise operational readiness — [MSMR Mental Health Diagnoses 2021-2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/08/01/MSMR-Mental-Health-2025)
- Adjustment, anxiety, and depressive disorders comprise the clinical burden majority of mental health care, accounting for 66.5% of all incident diagnoses in 2025 — [MSMR Mental Health Diagnoses 2021-2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/08/01/MSMR-Mental-Health-2025)
- In 2025, the unadjusted incidence rates of heat stroke and heat exhaustion among U.S. active component service members were 39.4 and 170.0 cases per 100,000 person-years, respectively — [MSMR Heat Illness Update 2021-2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/05/01/MSMR-Heat-Illness-2026)
- The unadjusted incidence rate of heat stroke increased 6.9% from 2024 to 2025, for the second year in a row, while the unadjusted annual incidence rate of heat exhaustion fell by 9.1% in 2025, following a 4-year increase from 2021 through 2024 — [MSMR Heat Illness Update 2021-2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/05/01/MSMR-Heat-Illness-2026)
- The percentages of in-theater medical encounters attributed to musculoskeletal system diseases increased from 21.1% to 31.4% from 2021 to 2025 — [MSMR Deployed Morbidity 2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/07/01/MSMR-Deployed-Morbidity-2025)
- Cold weather injury showed the largest increase in 2025 vs. 2024, with cases more than doubling (155% increase); cyclosporiasis also increased 100% — [MSMR Annual Review of Reportable Medical Events 2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/05/01/MSMR-RMEs-2025)
- Top 5 reportable medical events for active component service members in 2025: chlamydia, gonorrhea, norovirus, heat illness, and syphilis (unchanged from 2024) — [MSMR Annual Review RMEs 2025](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2026/05/01/MSMR-RMEs-2025)

### Inferences
- The simultaneous rise in musculoskeletal encounters (both garrison and deployed) and mental health diagnoses suggests compounding readiness impacts, as these two categories likely overlap significantly in the same service members.
- The continued rise in heat stroke incidence despite falling heat exhaustion rates may indicate either improved diagnostic capture of severe cases or an actual shift toward more severe heat injuries.

### Gaps
- Specific 2025 musculoskeletal injury incidence rates (per 100,000 person-years) were not available in search results; the growth data available is in visit volume/rate change rather than standardized incidence.
- Branch-specific breakdowns for mental health and musculoskeletal conditions in 2025 were not found in search snippets; the full MSMR articles behind health.mil were inaccessible due to network restrictions.

## What surveillance reports has DCPH-A published on injury prevention, behavioral health, and environmental health?

### Takeaway
DCPH-A publishes several regular surveillance products including the Active Duty Injury Surveillance Summary and the Surveillance of Suicidal Behavior report, and in February 2025 released a technical evaluation of the Army Reserve H2F Pilot Program. Their installation-level injury monitoring tools provide commanders with unit-specific data.

### Cited Findings
- DCPH-A Technical Report No. S.0094040.99-25 (February 2025) evaluated the USARC Holistic Health and Fitness Pilot Program, examining holistic health impacts through a baseline period (July 2020-December 2021) and follow-up during pilot implementation (January 2022-May 2023) — [DCPH-A H2F Pilot Evaluation Report](https://ph.health.mil/resources/cphe-ip-army-reserve-holistic-health-fitness-pilot-program-evaluation-report.pdf)
- The Active Duty Injury Surveillance Summary provides annual updates of injury-related medical encounters for non-deployed U.S. Soldiers, including rates over time for Active Duty and trainees, types and causes of hospitalizations and outpatient visits, and frequencies of injuries by body location and diagnosis — [DCPH-A Injury Prevention Epidemiology](https://ph.health.mil/topics/healthsurv/ip/Pages/default.aspx)
- The Surveillance of Suicidal Behavior publication reports on suicidal behavior among U.S. Army Active Component and Reserve Component Soldiers, examining risk factors including demographic, military, event characteristics, personal history, legal/administrative history, and mental health history — [DCPH-A Behavioral Health Reports](https://ph.health.mil/topics/healthsurv/bhe/Pages/default.aspx)
- DCPH-A provides Installation Injury Monitoring Tools that enable installation-level tracking of injury data — [DCPH-A Installation Injury Monitoring](https://ph.health.mil/topics/discond/ptsaip/Pages/Installation-Injury-Monitoring-Tools.aspx)

### Inferences
- DCPH-A's surveillance infrastructure is primarily Army-focused, which means comparable surveillance products for Navy, Air Force, and Marine Corps injury prevention may exist through different channels or may not exist at the same level of granularity.

### Gaps
- The most recent year of data in the Active Duty Injury Surveillance Summary was cited as 2022 in the 2024 report; whether a 2023 or 2024 data edition has been published could not be confirmed from search results.
- Specific environmental health surveillance reports from DCPH-A (e.g., on PFAS, burn pit exposures) were not returned in search results focused on 2025-2026.

## What DHA policy or surveillance updates address force health metrics?

### Takeaway
DHA's Armed Forces Health Surveillance Division (AFHSD) remains the primary DoD-wide surveillance entity, with the MSMR as its flagship publication. The FY2026 Defense Health Program budget request totals $39.2 billion, and DHA has published a new Data Strategy for FY2026-2030 to modernize health data integration across the Military Health System.

### Cited Findings
- The FY2026 request for the Unified Medical Budget is $64.0 billion (about 7.5% of DoD's total budget request), including $39.2 billion for the Defense Health Program, of which $10.7 billion would be for MTF care and $21.0 billion for "Private Sector Care" — [CRS Defense Primer: Military Health System (IF10530)](https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/details?prodcode=IF10530)
- DHA's health surveillance mission is organized into three branches: Epidemiology and Analysis, Global Emerging Infections Surveillance, and Integrated Biosurveillance — [DHA Health Surveillance](https://dha.mil/News/2025/09/08/15/32/Health-Surveillance-Fighting-against-infectious-disease-and-other-threats)
- The DHA Data Strategy for Fiscal Years 2026-2030 provides an actionable framework for achieving data advantage across the Military Health System — [DHA Data Strategy](https://dha.mil/Reference-Library/d/e/f/Defense-Health-Agency-Data-Strategy)
- DoD is prioritizing military medical personnel assignments to MTFs and aims to reattract at least 7% of beneficiary care to the MTFs by December 31, 2026 — [CRS Defense Primer: Military Health System (IF10530)](https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/details?prodcode=IF10530)

### Inferences
- The 7% beneficiary care reattraction goal to MTFs by end of 2026 signals a policy shift toward rebuilding organic military medical capacity after years of outsourcing to private sector care.

### Gaps
- Specific DHA policy memoranda or procedural instructions (DHA-PIs) on force health protection metrics issued in 2025-2026 were not identified in search results.

## What TBI Center of Excellence data is available on traumatic brain injury rates and outcomes?

### Takeaway
TBICoE tracks TBI worldwide numbers quarterly and annually. In 2024, DoD recorded 18,376 TBI cases (81.5% mild). In 2025 Q1, there were 3,003 new cases. Cumulative DoD TBI cases from 2000 through Q3 2025 total 533,519. Research links TBI to degraded medical readiness status.

### Cited Findings
- In 2024, there were 18,376 total TBI cases DoD-wide: 81.5% mild, 17.8% moderate, 0.4% severe, 0.3% penetrating (data current as of May 12, 2025) — [DoD TBI Worldwide Numbers 2024](https://www.health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2025/08/21/2024-DOD-Worldwide-Numbers-for-TBI)
- In 2023, there were 19,558 total TBI cases DoD-wide, with 81.2% classified as mild — [DoD TBI Worldwide Numbers 2023](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2025/04/18/2023-DOD-Worldwide-Numbers-for-TBI)
- In 2025 Q1, there were 3,003 total TBI cases: 86.3% mild, 13.3% moderate, 0.2% severe, 0.2% penetrating — [DoD TBI Worldwide Numbers 2025 Q1](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2025/08/22/2025-Q1-DOD-Worldwide-Numbers-for-TBI)
- Cumulative DoD TBI from 2000 through Q3 2025: 533,519 total cases (Penetrating: 5,915; Severe: 5,041; Moderate: 64,994; Mild: 436,895; Not Classifiable: 20,674) — [DoD TBI Worldwide Numbers 2000-2025 Q1](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2025/08/22/2000-2025-Q1-DOD-Worldwide-Numbers-for-TBI)
- In 2025 Q1, Army reported the highest number of TBI cases at 1,562 of the 3,003 total — [DoD TBI Worldwide Numbers 2025 Q1](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2025/08/22/2025-Q1-DOD-Worldwide-Numbers-for-TBI)
- 1.8% of Navy and Marine Corps personnel screened positive for TBI; those with TBI had significantly higher prevalence of not medically ready disposition (7.8%) vs. those without (3.7%) — [MSMR TBI and Readiness](https://health.mil/News/Articles/2025/01/01/MSMR-TBI-Readiness)
- TBICoE published a Research Review on Mild TBI and PTSD in January 2026 — [TBICoE Research Review](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Publications/2026/01/20/TBICoE-Research-Review-Mild-TBI-and-PTSD)

### Inferences
- The 2024 total (18,376) represents a ~6% decline from 2023 (19,558), though it is unclear whether this reflects a genuine decrease in TBI incidence or changes in diagnostic practices.
- The 2025 Q1 figure of 3,003 cases, if annualized (~12,012), would suggest a significant further decline, but quarterly data can be seasonal and incomplete.

### Gaps
- Full-year 2025 TBI data is not yet available (only Q1 data published as of the search date).
- Branch-specific annual breakdown for 2024 was not detailed in search snippets.
- TBI outcome data (recovery timelines, return-to-duty rates, long-term sequelae) were not found in the search results.

## What are current fitness test pass/fail rates across services where publicly reported?

### Takeaway
The Army transitioned to the Army Fitness Test (AFT) on June 1, 2025, replacing the ACFT. Pass standards are 300 points total (350 for 21 combat specialties). H2F program evaluations report a 23% increase in fitness test pass rates at participating units, but service-wide pass/fail rate statistics are not publicly reported in the search results.

### Cited Findings
- The Army Fitness Test (AFT) became the official physical fitness test of record for all Soldiers on June 1, 2025, replacing the ACFT — [Army Introduces New Fitness Test for 2025](https://www.army.mil/article/285501/army_introduces_new_fitness_test_for_2025)
- The AFT passing standard is 300 points total with a minimum of 60 points per event; combat specialties (21 designated MOSs) must score 350 total — [Army Introduces New Fitness Test for 2025](https://www.army.mil/article/285501/army_introduces_new_fitness_test_for_2025)
- The AFT is a five-event assessment: three-repetition maximum deadlift, hand-release push-up, sprint-drag-carry, plank, and two-mile run — [Army Introduces New Fitness Test for 2025](https://www.army.mil/article/285501/army_introduces_new_fitness_test_for_2025)
- Soldiers had until January 1, 2026 to meet AFT requirements without adverse actions — [Army Introduces New Fitness Test for 2025](https://www.army.mil/article/285501/army_introduces_new_fitness_test_for_2025)
- H2F program evaluations reported a 35% reduction in musculoskeletal injuries and a 23% increase in Army fitness test pass rates at participating units — [H2F Program Overview](https://www.serco.com/na/media-and-news/2025/how-the-armys-h2f-program-is-building-a-fitter-more-holistic-force)

### Inferences
- The transition from ACFT to AFT in mid-2025 creates a baseline discontinuity, making year-over-year pass/fail comparisons difficult during the transition period.
- The absence of publicly reported aggregate pass/fail data may be intentional, as services historically treat these as internal readiness metrics.

### Gaps
- No publicly reported service-wide AFT pass/fail rate data was found for 2025 or 2026.
- Navy PRT (Physical Readiness Test), Air Force fitness assessment, and Marine Corps PFT/CFT pass/fail rates for 2025-2026 were not found in search results.
- Historical ACFT pass/fail rates that could serve as a pre-AFT baseline were not found in official sources.

## What GAO or DoD IG reports address military health, fitness readiness, or H2F program effectiveness?

### Takeaway
GAO published major military readiness reports in both 2025 (GAO-25-108104) and 2026 (GAO-26-108888), plus defense health care reports addressing medical facility management and civilian partnership contributions to readiness. No specific GAO or DoD IG audit of the H2F program was found, though independent research demonstrates strong ROI (8.15:1) and significant injury/readiness benefits.

### Cited Findings
- GAO-25-108104 (March 2025): "Military Readiness: Implementing GAO's Recommendations Can Help DOD Address Persistent Challenges across Air, Sea, Ground, and Space Domains" — [GAO-25-108104](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-108104)
- GAO-26-108888 (March 2026): "Military Readiness: DOD Should Take Further Actions to Address Challenges Across the Air, Sea, Ground, and Space Domains" — [GAO-26-108888](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108888/index.html)
- GAO-25-107432 (April 2025): "Defense Health Care: Actions Needed to Address Long-Standing Management Challenges with Medical Facilities" — [GAO-25-107432](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107432/index.html)
- GAO-26-107677 (June 2026): "Defense Health Care: Actions Needed to Assess Civilian Partnerships' Contributions to Readiness" — [GAO-26-107677](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107677)
- DoD guidance emphasizes service members should obtain at least 7 hours of sleep for optimal performance and readiness, yet DoD surveys have found most service members report sleeping 6 or fewer hours per night for over a decade — [GAO-25-108104](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-108104)
- The ability to supply health care across the military health system remains constrained by insufficient numbers of medical personnel — [GAO-25-108104](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-108104)
- H2F program evaluation (published in Military Medicine): patients treated through H2F were evaluated an average of 33.8 days sooner than those in hospital clinics, showed significant improvements in clinical outcomes, and reported lower pain scores — [Military Medicine H2F Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/milmed/usaf419)
- A matched difference-in-differences study found H2F performance teams generated combined annual total economic value of $24.44 million per brigade (ROI = 8.15:1; 95% CI 7.17-9.27), with force-wide extrapolation yielding $5.28 billion in annual total Army returns — [Sports Medicine H2F ROI Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-026-02399-3)

### Inferences
- The absence of a specific GAO or DoD IG audit of H2F suggests the program has not yet been subject to independent federal oversight scrutiny, despite its significant scale and cost.
- GAO's repeated annual reports on military readiness challenges (2025, 2026) indicate persistent systemic issues that remain unresolved despite ongoing recommendations.

### Gaps
- No specific DoD IG report on the H2F program was found in search results.
- The DoD IG FY2025 oversight plan was identified but its specific health/fitness audit targets could not be determined from the search snippet.

## What National Academies consensus reports address military health or human performance?

### Takeaway
The National Academies published a major consensus report in August 2025 on military exposures and mental/behavioral/neurologic health outcomes among post-9/11 veterans, and has launched an ongoing study on military exposures (pesticides, jet fuels, PFAS) and neurological conditions. Additional 2025-2026 reports address military sexual trauma disability evaluations and autism care for military families.

### Cited Findings
- "Exploring Military Exposures and Mental, Behavioral, and Neurologic Health Outcomes Among Post-9/11 Veterans" (August 2025) assessed risk-conferring relationships between nine exposure categories (burn pits, dust/particulate matter, exhaust, fuels, incinerator emissions, metals, mold, radiation, solvents) and 14 health outcomes — [National Academies Military Exposures Report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/29219/Military_Exposures_Highlights.pdf)
- An ongoing National Academies study examines associations between six types of military-related exposures (pesticides, jet fuels, solvents, heavy metals, PFAS, air pollution) and selected neurological conditions — [National Academies Military Exposures and Neurologic Health](https://www.nationalacademies.org/projects/HMD-BPH-25-02/event/46672)
- "Improving Disability Evaluations for Veterans Who Have Experienced Military Sexual Trauma" published in 2026 — [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/topics/military-and-veterans)
- "The Comprehensive Autism Care Demonstration: Solutions for Military Families" published in 2025 — [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/topics/military-and-veterans)

### Inferences
- The National Academies' focus on exposure-outcome relationships provides a scientific evidence base that may inform future DHA surveillance priorities and VA benefits policy.

### Gaps
- No National Academies consensus report specifically addressing military human performance optimization, physical fitness, or tactical athlete health was found for 2025-2026.
- Whether the National Academies has studied the H2F model or military fitness testing could not be confirmed.

## Behavioral Health: Military Suicide Surveillance

### Takeaway
DoD released the CY2024 Annual Report on Suicide in the Military in March 2026. In CY2023, 523 service members died by suicide. The Q1 2025 Quarterly Suicide Report shows 117 suspected/confirmed suicide deaths across components.

### Cited Findings
- Calendar Year 2024 Annual Report on Suicide in the Military was released by DSPO in March 2026 — [DSPO CY2024 Report](https://www.dspo.mil/Portals/113/2026_CY/documents/DSPO_ReportonSuicide_CY24_20260317_508c.pdf)
- In CY2023, 523 servicemembers died by suicide: 363 Active Component, 69 Reserves, 91 National Guard — [DoD Suicide Report CY2023](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3964785/department-of-defense-releases-its-annual-report-on-suicide-in-the-military-for/)
- In Q1 2025, the Military Services reported 71 Active Component, 17 Reserve, and 29 National Guard deaths suspected or confirmed as suicides (117 total) — [DSPO Q1 2025 QSR](https://www.dspo.mil/Portals/113/Documents/QSR/TAB%20A_20250613_CY25-Qtr1%20QSR_508c.pdf)
- An MSMR study (October 2025) examined PTSD, TBI, and death by suicide specifically for EOD personnel — [MSMR PTSD/TBI/Suicide EOD](https://health.mil/Reference-Center/Reports/2025/10/06/PTSD-TBI--and-Death-by-Suicide-for-EOD-Personnel)

### Inferences
- The Q1 2025 figure of 117 deaths, if sustained across all four quarters, would project to approximately 468 annual suicide deaths, potentially lower than 2023's 523, though quarterly data is preliminary and subject to revision.

### Gaps
- Official CY2024 suicide counts/rates were not available in search snippets (the full PDF report was inaccessible due to network restrictions).
- Whether 2024 or 2025 data shows statistically significant trends relative to prior years could not be determined without full report access.
