# Tactical Athlete Human Performance Programs in Fire, EMS, and Law Enforcement (2025-2026)

## NFPA 1583 Status and the NFPA 1580 Consolidation

### Takeaway
NFPA 1583 (Health-Related Fitness Programs for Fire Department Members) has been retired as a standalone standard. The 2022 edition was its final individual release. Its content has been consolidated into the new NFPA 1580 (Standard for Emergency Responder Occupational Health and Wellness, 2025 edition), which also absorbs NFPA 1581, 1582, and 1584. A major change in the 2025 edition replaces the prior absolute 12 MET aerobic capacity threshold with age- and biological-sex-adjusted percentiles based on the American College of Sports Medicine's general population classification system.

### Cited Findings
- NFPA 1580, 2025 edition, consolidates NFPA 1581 (infection control), 1582 (medical programs), 1583 (fitness programs), and 1584 (rehabilitation) into a single standard. — [NFPA 1580 2025 Edition listing](https://www.fire-police-ems.com/NFPA1580-2025.shtml)
- The 2025 edition replaces the old absolute 12 MET aerobic capacity threshold with ACSM general population, percentile-based classification system adjusted for age and biological sex. — [NFPA 1580 redraws the line on firefighter fitness](https://www.firerescue1.com/health-wellness/nfpa-1580-redraws-the-line-on-firefighter-fitness)
- A peer-reviewed article in Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism (2025) examines how the revised NFPA 1580 alters firefighter fit-for-duty status across age: "One job, one standard." — [cdnsciencepub.com](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/apnm-2025-0207)
- The 2025 edition features new recommended age/gender adjustments for cardiorespiratory fitness and aerobic capacity, and updated requirements related to fitness and rehabilitation. — [NFPA 1580 transition explainer](https://phsmobile.com/nfpa-1580/)
- The 2022 editions of NFPA 1581, 1582, 1583, and 1584 are the final standalone editions of those standards. — [NFPA 1583 Standard Development page](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-1583-standard-development/1583)

### Inferences
- Fire departments that previously relied on the standalone NFPA 1583 for fitness program design will need to transition to NFPA 1580 (2025) and may need to recalibrate fitness assessments from the absolute 12 MET standard to the new percentile-based model.
- The shift to percentile-based norms may make fitness standards more equitable across age groups but could generate debate about whether percentile-based thresholds adequately reflect the absolute physical demands of firefighting.

### Gaps
- Specific percentile cutoffs and how they map to prior 12 MET thresholds were not available in detail from the sources accessed.
- Adoption rate of NFPA 1580 (2025) across fire departments is not yet documented.

## IAFF and IAFC Wellness-Fitness Programs

### Takeaway
The IAFF and IAFC jointly manage the Wellness-Fitness Initiative (WFI), which is undergoing a major revision with a draft expected to be completed by late 2025 and distribution/implementation beginning in early 2026. The IAFF's Fit to Thrive (F2T) program, peer support training, and behavioral health initiatives are the primary member-facing programs.

### Cited Findings
- The Fire Service Joint Labor Management Wellness-Fitness Initiative (WFI) is a collaboration between the IAFF and IAFC to standardize wellness and fitness guidelines for all fire departments, covering physical, mental, and emotional well-being. — [IAFC WFI page](https://www.iafc.org/topics-and-tools/safety-health/wellness-fitness-task-force)
- The WFI Task Force aims to complete a draft of the updated Wellness-Fitness Initiative by 2025, with plans for distribution and implementation beginning in early 2026. — [IAFC blog post, February 2025](https://www.iafc.org/blogs/blog/iafc/2025/02/17/fire-service-joint-labor-management-wellness-fitness-initiative-(wfi))
- The IAFF and IAFC are revising the wellness-fitness program to improve firefighter health. — [Fire and Safety Journal Americas](https://fireandsafetyjournalamericas.com/iaff-and-iafc-revise-wellness-fitness-program-to-improve-firefighter-health/)
- The IAFF Fit to Thrive (F2T) program is designed to support the WFI by helping firefighters be more active using a peer-to-peer model. Members can network globally with like-minded individuals. — [IAFF F2T page](https://www.iaff.org/fit-to-thrive/)
- After completing IAFF peer support training, members become IAFF Trained Peer Supporters with knowledge and skills to support peers, educate about behavioral health, and serve as a bridge to behavioral health programs and community resources. — [IAFF WFI page](https://www.iaff.org/wfi/)
- The IAFF/IAFC Peer Fitness Trainer (PFT) grant program provides funding support for departments to develop peer fitness training capacity. — [IAFF PFT Grant Guidance PDF](https://www.iaff.org/wp-content/uploads/Departments/Grants_Department/28233_PFT-Grant-Guidance.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2025-2026 WFI revision represents the most significant update to the fire service's foundational wellness framework in years, potentially incorporating updated evidence on cancer, cardiac risk, and behavioral health.
- The peer-to-peer model (both F2T for fitness and IAFF Peer Supporters for behavioral health) reflects a deliberate strategy to leverage trust within the fire service culture rather than relying solely on external clinicians.

### Gaps
- The specific content changes in the WFI draft revision were not publicly available at the time of research.
- Quantitative data on how many departments have adopted the WFI or F2T programs was not found.

## NSCA Tactical Strength and Conditioning (TSAC) Certification and Research

### Takeaway
The NSCA's Tactical Strength and Conditioning Facilitator (TSAC-F) certification remains the primary credential for professionals training tactical athletes across military, fire, EMS, and law enforcement. The certification covers exercise science, nutrition, exercise technique, assessment, program design, and wellness intervention. The NSCA's TSAC Report continues to publish applied research relevant to tactical populations.

### Cited Findings
- The TSAC-F credential is for professionals training law enforcement, fire and rescue, military, protective services, and other emergency personnel, with goals of promoting wellness, boosting performance, and lessening injury risk. — [NSCA TSAC-F Exam Description](https://www.nsca.com/certification/tsac-f/tactical-strength-and-conditioning-facilitator-exam-description/)
- The TSAC-F exam covers: Exercise Science (20%), Nutrition (8%), Exercise Technique (20%), Assessment and Evaluation (13%), Program Design (21%), and Wellness Intervention (10%). Passing score is a scaled 70+. — [NSCA TSAC-F Exam Description](https://www.nsca.com/certification/tsac-f/tactical-strength-and-conditioning-facilitator-exam-description/)
- Prerequisites include: 18+ years old, high school diploma or equivalent, current CPR/AED certification. The certification renews every 3 years. — [NSCA TSAC-F Exam Prerequisites](https://www.nsca.com/certification/tsac-f/tsac-f-exam-prerequisites/)
- Upcoming TSAC Practitioners Course scheduled for October 12-15, 2026 in Lakewood, CO ($1,200). — [NSCA TSAC Practitioners Course](https://www.nsca.com/education/courses/tsac-practitioners-course/tsac-practitioners-course/)
- The DOD Civilian COOL, Navy COOL, and Coast Guard COOL programs all recognize the TSAC-F as a credentialing option, indicating military-civilian cross-sector relevance. — [DOD Civilian COOL TSAC-F](https://www.cool.osd.mil/dciv/credential/index.html?cert=tsac-f6556)
- East Texas A&M University offers a Tactical Performance and Resilience Certificate program. — [ETAMU Tactical Performance and Resilience Certificate](https://www.etamu.edu/tprcert/)

### Inferences
- The TSAC-F's recognition across DOD COOL programs demonstrates formal convergence between military and first responder human performance credentialing.
- The inclusion of wellness intervention (10%) and nutrition (8%) in the TSAC-F exam content indicates movement beyond pure strength and conditioning toward holistic human performance.

### Gaps
- Specific 2025-2026 updates to the TSAC-F exam content or TSAC Report publications were not found in detail.
- Total number of TSAC-F certified professionals currently active was not available.

## FBI Fitness Standards and Federal Law Enforcement Requirements

### Takeaway
The FBI updated its Physical Fitness Test (PFT) in late 2025, replacing sit-ups with pull-ups and lowering the overall passing threshold from 12 to 10 points. Other federal agencies (DEA, Secret Service, US Marshals) maintain separate fitness testing protocols with broadly similar components. The Cooper Institute norms remain foundational for many state and local LE agencies.

### Cited Findings
- The FBI PFT was updated in late 2025: pull-ups/chin-ups replaced sit-ups; passing total lowered from 12 to 10 points. Four events administered in fixed order with no more than 5 minutes rest between: maximum continuous pull-ups/chin-ups, timed 300-meter sprint, maximum continuous push-ups, timed 1.5-mile run. Must score at least 1 point per event. — [Stew Smith Fitness](https://www.stewsmithfitness.com/blogs/news/2025-fbi-physical-fitness-test-new-standards-and-requirements-pullups-added)
- Male candidates need 2-3 pull-ups for minimum passing (1 point); female candidates need 1 pull-up for 1 point. — [Stew Smith Fitness](https://www.stewsmithfitness.com/blogs/news/2025-fbi-physical-fitness-test-new-standards-and-requirements-pullups-added)
- The DEA fitness test includes push-ups, sit-ups, 300m sprint, 1.5-mile run with pass/fail requirements (no point system). — [Special Agents.org](https://www.specialagents.org/special-agent-fitness)
- The Secret Service Applicant Physical Abilities Test (APAT) consists of push-ups, sit-ups, Illinois Agility Run, and 1.5-mile run. Minimum cumulative score of 20 points with no zeros required. — [Secret Service APAT page](https://www.secretservice.gov/careers/APAT)
- The US Marshals Physical Efficiency Battery (PEB) consists of 1.5-mile run, 1-minute push-up test, and 1-minute sit-up test with age/gender-adjusted minimums. Incumbent FEOs must retest bi-annually. — [US Marshals Fitness Standards](https://www.usmarshals.gov/careers/enforcement-officer/federal-enforcement-officer-fitness-standards)

### Inferences
- The FBI's replacement of sit-ups with pull-ups signals a shift toward upper body pulling strength assessment, which more closely maps to operational demands (climbing, pulling, grappling).
- Federal LE agencies show broad commonality in tested fitness domains (cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength/endurance) but differ in scoring methodology and specific events.

### Gaps
- ATF-specific fitness standards were not found in search results.
- Whether other federal agencies are considering similar pull-up additions or scoring changes was not documented.
- Ongoing/recurring fitness requirements for incumbent federal agents (beyond hiring) are inconsistently documented across agencies.

## Cooper Institute and State/Local LE Fitness Standards

### Takeaway
The Cooper Institute remains the most widely referenced fitness assessment framework for state and local law enforcement agencies, providing age- and gender-normed standards across core fitness components. Many state academies and local departments set minimum requirements using Cooper percentile cutoffs (commonly the 25th-40th percentile).

### Cited Findings
- Cooper Law Enforcement Standards assess dynamic strength (push-ups and sit-ups in one minute), cardiorespiratory fitness (1.5-mile run), anaerobic fitness (300M run), explosive power (vertical jump), and absolute strength (bench press). Standards are age and gender specific. — [Cooper Standards](https://www.scribd.com/document/38633574/Cooper-Standards)
- Results are categorized by age group (20-29, 30-39, 40-49, 50-59, 60+) and placed into percentile rankings representing a cross-section of general and law enforcement populations. — [VALOR for Blue FAQ](https://www.valorforblue.org/Clearinghouse/697/Frequently-Asked-Questions-Regarding-Fitness-Standards-in-Law-Enforcement)
- Pennsylvania's Municipal Police Officers' Education and Training Commission uses Cooper-based physical fitness assessments for basic police officer training. — [PA MPOETC Physical Fitness](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/mpoetc/programs/training/basic-police-officer-training/physical-fitness)
- New York State Police fitness requirements are based on Cooper Institute norms. — [NY State Police Fitness Requirements](https://joinstatepolice.ny.gov/fitness-requirements)
- The Cooper Institute offers specific law enforcement fitness courses for agencies. — [Police Magazine](https://www.policemag.com/news/cooper-institute-offers-law-enforcement-fitness-course)

### Inferences
- Cooper Institute norms serve as the de facto national standard for LE fitness testing at the state/local level, providing legal defensibility through their validated, population-normed methodology.
- The variation in minimum percentile requirements across agencies creates significant inconsistency in what "physically fit for duty" means across jurisdictions.

### Gaps
- No major 2025-2026 updates to Cooper Institute LE fitness norms were found.
- Comprehensive data on what percentage of US LE agencies use Cooper norms vs. alternative standards was not available.

## Firefighter, EMS, and LE Health Research: Cardiac Events, Cancer, PTSD

### Takeaway
Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of firefighter line-of-duty deaths. Cancer risk in firefighters continues to be a major research focus, with the NIOSH Fire Fighter Cancer Cohort Study ongoing. PTSD affects approximately 22% of fire service members at some point in their careers. Research in 2025-2026 is expanding to integrate human factors into fatality investigations and to better understand links between noise exposure, chemical exposure, and cardiac/cancer outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of line-of-duty mortality among firefighters, with sudden cardiac death occurring disproportionately during emergency operations, particularly fire suppression. Firefighters exhibit high prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors: hypertension (10-44%), obesity (14-60%), dyslipidemia (20-56%), metabolic syndrome (~22%). — [Mortada et al., 2026, Cardiology in Review](https://doi.org/10.1177/11795468261479323)
- A 2025 study in the Journal of Occupational and Environmental Hygiene examined spectral frequency and broadband noise analysis of the firefighter environment and potential cardiac health implications. — [JOEH 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15459624.2025.2086017)
- 22% of fire service members will meet criteria for PTSD at some point during their careers. Experienced firefighters face higher rates of PTSD compared to new recruits. — [Senator Gillibrand behavioral health statistics report, February 2025](https://www.gillibrand.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Behavioral-Health-Statistics-in-the-Fire-Service.pdf)
- The NIOSH Fire Fighter Cancer Cohort Study (FFCCS) continues to publish findings and media coverage on occupational cancer risk. — [FFCCS Publications](https://www.ffccs.org/publications)
- NIOSH Fire Fighter Fatality Investigation and Prevention Program has undertaken efforts since 2023 to collect human factors data, with formal data collection estimated to begin in summer 2026. Over 750 independent LODD investigations have been conducted since 1998. — [NIOSH FFFIPP](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/firefighters/fffipp/ongoing-reports.html)
- A 2025 PubMed article examined incorporating human factors during firefighter fatality investigations, analyzing 33 public feedback responses from fire departments and academic institutions across 18 US states and several Canadian provinces. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41786438/)

### Inferences
- The integration of human factors (fatigue, sleep, stress, cognitive load) into NIOSH fatality investigations marks a significant shift from purely mechanical/procedural investigation toward holistic occupational health analysis.
- The high prevalence of modifiable cardiovascular risk factors suggests significant opportunity for human performance programs to reduce cardiac LODD through targeted fitness, nutrition, and health monitoring interventions.

### Gaps
- EMS-specific injury and health outcome research was sparse in search results; most research focuses on firefighters or combines fire/EMS.
- Law enforcement-specific cancer risk research was not found in this search (most occupational cancer research in tactical populations focuses on firefighters).
- Longitudinal outcomes data from departments implementing comprehensive HP programs was not found.

## Tactical Athlete Nutrition, Sleep, and Mental Performance

### Takeaway
The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) published a 2022 position stand on tactical athlete nutrition that remains the foundational reference. Research in 2025 continues to emphasize convergence between military and first responder nutritional needs, with caloric deficits of up to 3,000 kcal/day during high-demand operations. Sleep banking, shift schedule optimization, and mental performance programs are gaining traction but remain inconsistently implemented.

### Cited Findings
- The ISSN position stand on tactical athlete nutrition (2022, PMC9261739) provides evidence-based nutritional guidelines for military, fire, law enforcement, and other tactical personnel, noting unique demands including irregular schedules, heat exposure, sleep deprivation, and limited food access. — [ISSN Position Stand (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9261739/)
- A 2025 article in Sports Medicine, "Nutrition for Tactical Athletes: Insights, Applications and Research Gaps," updates the evidence base for tactical population nutritional needs. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02363-7)
- A 2025 mixed-methods study examined promoting performance nutrition behaviors among tactical athletes. — [PubMed (PMC12051564)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12051564/)
- The Army's Performance Triad framework (Sleep, Activity, Nutrition) has influenced first responder wellness programming, emphasizing that soldiers/tactical athletes require 7-8 hours of quality sleep every 24 hours to maintain mental edge. — [Army Performance Triad Guide](https://ireland.tricare.mil/Portals/66/P3%20Guide.pdf)
- "Sleep banking" — purposefully getting more sleep before high operational tempo — is recommended as a strategy that can be started two weeks in advance. — [Training & Conditioning](https://training-conditioning.com/article/tactical-approach-to-sleep-to-enhance-performance/)
- Approximate caloric deficit can reach 3,000 kcal/day during periods of military training and operations; similar deficits are likely during extended firefighting operations. — [ISSN Position Stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9261739/)

### Inferences
- The Army's Performance Triad (Sleep, Activity, Nutrition) framework provides a ready-made model for first responder agencies to adapt, and there is evidence of increasing cross-pollination between military and fire/EMS/LE human performance approaches.
- Shift work remains the fundamental obstacle to sleep optimization in fire/EMS/LE populations, making interventions fundamentally different from military contexts where sleep schedules can sometimes be controlled.

### Gaps
- Specific fire/EMS/LE department-level nutrition programs with published outcomes were not found.
- Mental performance training (cognitive enhancement, decision-making under stress) programs specific to first responders were not well-documented in search results beyond peer support and behavioral health.

## Wearable Technology and Monitoring in Fire/EMS/LE

### Takeaway
Wearable physiological monitoring technology is rapidly advancing in the first responder space, with devices tracking heart rate, body temperature, blood oxygen, and hazardous gas exposure. The DC Fire and EMS Department is piloting cardiac monitoring wearables. FirstNet (AT&T) is promoting the "Connected Responder" concept. Smart PPE with embedded sensors is emerging as a major trend for 2025-2026.

### Cited Findings
- Wearable technologies and smart PPE are advancing with embedded sensors that monitor heat stress, toxic gas exposure, and vital signs while tracking responder location in complex or smoke-filled environments. — [Fire Engineering 2025 Outlook](https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting-equipment/fire-ems-outlook-2025-challenges-innovations-and-strategic-shifts/)
- The District of Columbia Fire and EMS Department is testing a wearable device to track cardiac health of its training class, motivated by sudden cardiac events being the top cause of firefighter death. — [Fire Apparatus Magazine](https://www.fireapparatusmagazine.com/fire-apparatus/wearable-technology-monitors-firefighter-health-and-safety/)
- FirstNet (AT&T) is promoting the "Connected Responder" era, integrating wearable biometric monitoring with network infrastructure for first responders. — [AT&T Blog, 2026](https://about.att.com/blogs/2026/firstnet-empowers-connected-responder-era.html)
- Companies like Ascent Integrated Tech provide platforms tracking firefighters on scene with real-time biometric data (heart rate, body temperature, hazardous gas detection) for incident commanders. — [Fire Apparatus Magazine](https://www.fireapparatusmagazine.com/fire-apparatus/wearable-technology-monitors-firefighter-health-and-safety/)
- A PMC study (2023) explored first responders' use and perceptions of continuous health and environmental monitoring, finding general acceptance with privacy and practicality concerns. — [PMC10048923](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10048923/)

### Inferences
- The convergence of wearable sensor technology, cellular network infrastructure (FirstNet), and human performance science creates opportunities for real-time physiological monitoring that was previously only available in military special operations or elite sports contexts.
- Privacy and union concerns around continuous physiological monitoring will likely be a significant implementation barrier, particularly in unionized fire departments.

### Gaps
- Specific outcomes data from departments that have implemented wearable monitoring programs was not available.
- LE-specific wearable monitoring adoption (beyond body cameras) was not well-documented in the sources found.
- Cost and scalability data for department-wide wearable monitoring deployments was not found.

## Law Enforcement Mental Health and Wellness Programs

### Takeaway
The Law Enforcement Mental Health and Wellness Act (LEMHWA) provides federal grant funding (approximately $9 million in FY26) for LE agencies to implement mental health programs including peer support, suicide prevention, stress reduction, and family services. Additional legislation (HR 2502, 2025) proposes $10 million annually for mental health crisis response training.

### Cited Findings
- The LEMHWA program provides approximately $9 million in FY26 funding with awards up to $250,000 across three categories. Eligible activities include counseling/clinical supports, peer mentoring/peer support teams, suicide prevention initiatives, stress reduction resources, leadership training, and police family services. — [COPS/DOJ LEMHWA page](https://cops.usdoj.gov/lemhwa)
- FY26 LEMHWA funding opportunity closed August 5, 2026. — [Grants.gov listing](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/e2e9ff48-9226-4352-ad27-a0b7850b5c07)
- HR 2502, the "Law Enforcement Training for Mental Health Crisis Response Act of 2025," proposes up to $10 million annually for agencies to train officers in evidence-based mental health crisis response techniques, aiming to reduce injuries and fatalities for both officers and individuals in crisis. — [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2502/text)

### Inferences
- Federal investment in LE mental health is growing through both direct wellness funding (LEMHWA) and crisis response training (HR 2502), though total funding remains modest relative to the ~900,000 sworn officers in the US.
- The emphasis on peer support across both fire and LE sectors represents a shared tactical approach to behavioral health that bridges both populations.

### Gaps
- Outcome data from LEMHWA-funded programs was not found.
- State-level LE wellness mandates or model programs beyond federal funding were not explored in depth.

## Military-First Responder Human Performance Convergence

### Takeaway
There is clear and growing convergence between military and first responder human performance approaches, evidenced by the NSCA TSAC-F credential being recognized across DOD COOL programs, the Army Performance Triad framework influencing first responder wellness design, the ISSN tactical athlete nutrition position stand covering both populations, and emerging academic programs (e.g., East Texas A&M Tactical Performance and Resilience Certificate) that serve both populations.

### Cited Findings
- The DOD Civilian COOL, Navy COOL, and Coast Guard COOL programs all recognize the NSCA TSAC-F as a credentialing pathway. — [DOD Civilian COOL](https://www.cool.osd.mil/dciv/credential/index.html?cert=tsac-f6556); [Navy COOL](https://www.cool.osd.mil/usn/credential/index.html?cert=tsac-f6556); [Coast Guard COOL](https://www.cool.osd.mil/uscg/credential/index.html?cert=tsac-f6556)
- The ISSN tactical athlete nutrition position stand explicitly addresses military, law enforcement, fire and rescue, and other tactical populations as a unified category with shared nutritional challenges. — [ISSN Position Stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9261739/)
- The Army Performance Triad (Sleep, Activity, Nutrition) framework has been adapted by first responder agencies as a model for holistic wellness programming. — [Performance Triad Guide](https://ireland.tricare.mil/Portals/66/P3%20Guide.pdf)
- East Texas A&M University offers a Tactical Performance and Resilience Certificate serving both military and first responder populations. — [ETAMU](https://www.etamu.edu/tprcert/)
- Fire and EMS departments are integrating wellness programs that include peer support networks, real-time vital sign tracking, and shift designs aimed at reducing fatigue — approaches pioneered in military human performance optimization. — [Fire Engineering 2025 Outlook](https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting-equipment/fire-ems-outlook-2025-challenges-innovations-and-strategic-shifts/)

### Inferences
- The "tactical athlete" concept has matured to the point where military and first responder populations are increasingly served by the same credentialing, research, and programming frameworks, though implementation contexts differ significantly (shift work vs. deployment cycles, union environments vs. military chain of command).
- The convergence creates opportunities for cross-sector knowledge transfer, particularly from the military's more established and better-funded human performance programs to first responder agencies.

### Gaps
- Specific examples of fire/EMS/LE departments that have directly adopted military HP program models (e.g., Special Operations human performance programs adapted for fire service) were not found.
- Comparative effectiveness research between military-origin and fire/LE-developed wellness programs was not available.
