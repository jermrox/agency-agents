# Demographics and psychographics of screenless recovery-wearable buyers and the "subscription-fatigued" segment (2024–2026)

Research date: 2026-09-30. Scope: WHOOP, Oura, Garmin recovery users, Amazfit Helio Strap, Polar Loop; overlap with US Instagram/Facebook audiences; HYROX/CrossFit/hybrid; life-situation segments; ad/waitlist response. US first, UK/EU second.

Method note for the writer: many primary domains (pewresearch.org, rockhealth.com, whoop.com, businesswire.com, dcrainmaker.com, techradar.com, wareable.com, jmir.org, researchgate.net, techcrunch.com, thenationalnews.com, futurefemhealth.com, sacra.com, circana.com) were blocked by this environment's egress proxy, so full-text verification was not possible. Figures below were taken from search-result excerpts of those pages plus accessible secondary coverage. Each figure is labelled **[company]** (company-reported), **[survey]** (independent survey/academic), **[estimate]** (analyst/aggregator estimate) or **[review/press]** (journalist or reviewer). Where an aggregator's number could not be traced to a primary source it is flagged **[unverified aggregator]** and should be treated as low confidence.

## KQ1. What do WHOOP, Oura, Garmin, Amazfit and Polar disclose (or third parties estimate) about their user base: age, gender, income, country, sport?

### Takeaway
Oura has flipped to a female-majority base (59% women per Oura's own 2024/25 disclosure; a 2026 IPO-filing-based report puts it as high as ~72%), skews 25–44, is >80% US by hardware sales, and its fastest-growing cohort is women in their early 20s. WHOOP remains male-majority (~55–57% male) and 25–44 core, but women are now ~1 in 4 new members and women 40+ have tripled since Jan 2025; the oft-repeated ">70% of US members earn $100K+" could not be traced to a primary source. Garmin's core is affluent 30–55 (~40% female per aggregator estimates). Amazfit and Polar publish no buyer demographics.

### Inferences
- The two incumbents have segmented the market by gender and motive: Oura = female-majority, wellness/sleep/cycle framing; WHOOP = male-majority, strain/performance framing but rapidly adding women 40+. A no-subscription screenless band sits between them and can credibly target both, but the creative must be split by cohort rather than one "athlete" message.
- The 25–44 bracket is the core for every brand with data (Oura, WHOOP web audience, Garmin). 18–24 women are the fastest-growing Oura cohort; 40+ women are the fastest-growing WHOOP cohort.
- Income data is the weakest link: the only figures are unverified aggregator claims ($100K+ for WHOOP, $120K+ median for Garmin). Use general wearable-owner income data (KQ5) instead of brand-specific claims.
- Helio Strap's 2025 stock-outs are the strongest hard evidence that a sub-$100–200, no-subscription screenless band has real US demand.

### Gaps
- No primary WHOOP disclosure of member age, gender or income split was found; the "70% >$100K" figure is unsourced.
- Could not open Oura's S-1 to confirm the 72% female / <20% international figures.
- No Amazfit Helio Strap or Polar Loop buyer demographics exist publicly.
- No Garmin-published user demographics; aggregator figures only.
- UK/EU brand demographics: nothing beyond Oura's 20-country retail footprint and WHOOP's GCC comment.

## KQ2. US Instagram and Facebook demographics (2025–2026) and overlap with the wearable buyer

### Takeaway
Instagram's US adult reach is 80% at ages 18–29 and 62% at 30–49, skews female (55% of women vs 44% of men use it), and its US audience is concentrated at 18–34 (~55%). Facebook is broader and older but still 25–34-heavy (25.9% of US audience) and 53% female. Both platforms therefore over-index on exactly the 25–44 female-leaning wellness cohort Oura owns, while WHOOP's 25–34 male core is well-covered on Instagram.

### Cited Findings
- Pew (survey of 5,022 US adults, Feb 5–Jun 18 2025, published Nov 20 2025): Instagram used by 80% of 18–29s, 62% of 30–49s, 40% of 50–64s, 19% of 65+; 55% of women vs 44% of men **[survey]** — [Pew Research, Americans' Social Media Use 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/); [Pew PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/11/PI_2025.11.20_Social-Media-Use_REPORT.pdf); [Pew fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)
- US Instagram audience age distribution (ad-reach data, 2026): 18–24 = 26.5%, 25–34 = 28.3%, 35–44 = 19.4%, 45–54 = 12.2%, 55–64 = 7.8%, 65+ = 5.8% **[estimate: Meta ad-planning data via DataReportal/Statista]** — [Statista US Instagram by age](https://www.statista.com/statistics/398166/us-instagram-user-age-distribution/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/instagram-stats/); [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-demographics/)
- US Instagram gender: 55.1% women / 44.9% men (Aug 2026); other 2026 sources give 55.4% / 44.6%; globally ~50.6% male / 49.4% female **[estimate: Meta ad-reach data]** — [Statista US Instagram by gender](https://www.statista.com/statistics/530498/instagram-users-in-the-us-by-gender/); [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/instagram-users); [Phyllo](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/instagram-demographics-audience-creators-stats)
- US Facebook audience (June 2026): 25–34 = 25.9% (largest), 18–24 = 18.6%, 35–54 combined = 36.9%; 25–44 "just over half"; women 53% **[estimate: Meta ad-reach data]** — [Statista US Facebook by age](https://www.statista.com/statistics/187549/facebook-distribution-of-users-age-group-usa/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/facebook-statistics/)
- Rock Health 2025 (N=8,000, Census-matched): Gen Z has the highest wearable/connected-device ownership of any generation (66%); owners are "younger, wealthier, more urban, healthier, more likely commercially insured" **[survey]** — [Rock Health 2025 wearables insight](https://rockhealth.com/insights/whats-your-score-insights-on-wearables-and-connected-devices-from-rock-healths-2025-consumer-adoption-survey/); [Fierce Healthcare summary](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/health-wearable-ownership-33-past-decade-rock-health-survey)
- eMarketer: Gen Z is the fastest-growing generation among smart-wearable users **[estimate]** — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/gen-z-fastest-growing-generation-among-smart-wearable-users)
- Strava (2025 Year in Sport): more than half of Gen Z plan to use Strava more in 2026 while most say they will use Instagram and TikTok the same amount or less **[survey, Strava-commissioned]** — [Strava press release](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025)

### Inferences
- Overlap is strongest for the 25–44 bracket: it is ~48% of US Instagram, ~50%+ of US Facebook, and the core of every recovery-wearable brand's base.
- Instagram's female skew (55%) mirrors Oura's base; a screenless band can reach Oura-style buyers on Instagram and WHOOP-style 25–34 men on Instagram and Reels. Facebook adds the 45–54 bracket that WHOOP says is its fastest-growing female cohort (women 40+ tripled).
- Gen Z's declining stated Instagram intent (Strava) suggests the 18–24 female Oura cohort may be more efficiently reached via Reels and creators than feed placements.

### Gaps
- Pew does not publish Instagram/Facebook use by income within the 2025 report excerpts found; the full PDF was not retrievable.
- No source directly cross-tabulates wearable ownership with Instagram use.

## KQ3. Who is the "subscription-fatigued" buyer? Surveys on fee fatigue, cancellation intent, buy-once preference; Helio Strap / Polar Loop buyers and switchers

### Takeaway
No formal survey quantifying WHOOP/Oura subscription fatigue or cancellation intent was found. The evidence is (a) structural: only 32% of US wearable owners have any attached subscription (Rock Health 2025); (b) event-driven: WHOOP's May 2025 upgrade-fee backlash and 2026 pricing controversy, followed by Helio Strap ($99, no sub) selling out through Q3–Q4 2025; and (c) reviewer consensus that the no-subscription positioning is the main draw of Helio Strap and Polar Loop. Amazfit/Polar do not disclose who switched.

### Cited Findings
- Only 32% of US wearable owners have an attached subscription (Rock Health 2025, N=8,000) **[survey]** — [Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/insights/whats-your-score-insights-on-wearables-and-connected-devices-from-rock-healths-2025-consumer-adoption-survey/)
- 92% of US wearable purchase intenders are willing to pay extra for a device with at least one health feature; nearly 50% of US internet households own and use a wearable **[survey: Parks Associates]** — [Parks Associates](https://www.parksassociates.com/blogs/connected-health-pr/nearly-50-of-us-internet-households-own-and-use-a-wearable)
- General subscription sentiment (not wearable-specific): 54% of consumers think subscriptions provide better value than one-time purchases **[survey aggregation]** — [Marketing LTB](https://marketingltb.com/blog/statistics/subscription-statistics/)
- WHOOP upgrade backlash (May 2025): WHOOP initially charged a $49 upgrade fee to existing members for WHOOP 5.0/MG, breaking a prior free-upgrade promise; after "a huge user backlash" it reversed and offered free upgrades **[press]** — [TechCrunch May 11 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/11/fitness-tracker-whoop-faces-unhappy-customers-over-upgrade-policy); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/fitness-trackers/whoop-has-broken-a-promise-on-free-hardware-upgrades-and-users-arent-pleased)

- Cancellation friction cited by reviewers: WHOOP requires a ~5-minute exit survey and cannot be cancelled in-app; when cancelled the band stops working and historical data becomes inaccessible; Oura also gates full data behind membership; "$25–$40/month indefinitely... after two years you have spent $600–$960 and own nothing" **[review]** — [Dan Curtis Substack](https://dancurtisgo.substack.com/p/i-wore-an-apple-watch-oura-whoop); [Hiyd 5-year cost breakdown](https://hiyd.ai/blogs/news/whoop-five-year-cost-breakdown); [BetterVitals on Oura membership](https://www.bettervitals.com/learn/oura-ring-subscription-worth-it-2026)

- Helio Strap demand: Zepp management said supply "struggled to keep up during peak demand periods" and they under-manufactured for Q3–Q4 2025 **[company]** — [Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-zepp-healths-q4-2025-revenue-surges-43-93CH-4561628)
- Polar Loop: launched Sept 3 2025 at $199.99, screenless, no subscription; DC Rainmaker: "Actually a Whoop competitor?"; the5krunner: "Accurate, but not a Whoop competitor"; TechRadar: "good on heart rate, but a software let-down"; reviewers note Loop "requires constant care and feeding" vs WHOOP's automatic background operation, improved by Nov 2025 firmware **[review]** — [DC Rainmaker first thoughts](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2025/09/polar-loop-first-thoughts-actually-competitor.html); [DC Rainmaker June 2025 preview](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2025/06/polar-whoop-consumer-competitor-september.html); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/09/30/polar-loop-review-whoop-competitor/); [TechRadar Polar Loop review](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/fitness-trackers/polar-loop-review); [Gadgets & Wearables comparison](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2025/09/19/polar-loop-vs-amazfit-helio-strap/)
- 2026 landscape: alternatives now include Garmin, Google (Fitbit Air at ~$99), Oura, Polar Loop ($199), Amazfit Helio Strap ($99.99); WHOOP One $199/yr, Peak $239/yr, Life $359/yr **[review]** — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-best-whoop-alternatives-2026/); [Gadgets & Wearables best screenless 2026](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/07/26/best-screenless-fitness-tracker/); [TechRadar 3-way comparison](https://techradar.com/health-fitness/fitness-trackers/amazfit-helio-strap-vs-polar-loop-vs-whoop-5-0-which-should-you-buy)
- Oura counter-signal: 12-month retention "in the high 80s" while "other wearables struggle to keep even a third of users" (Oura CCO) **[company]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/oura-is-winning-young-women-and-losing-gym-rats-and-its-fine-with-that/)

### Inferences
- The subscription-fatigued buyer is best defined behaviourally, not demographically: current or lapsed WHOOP members (the one brand with a hard paywall), price-sensitive 25–44 men who want strain/recovery without $199–359/yr, and Oura-curious women who balk at $5.99/mo on top of a $349+ ring. WHOOP's own 2025 upgrade-fee episode created the clearest "moment" of fatigue.
- Reviewer consensus is that the no-sub alternatives win on price but lose on app polish and automation; a new entrant's ad message should pair "no subscription" with "it just works in the background" to pre-empt the Polar Loop critique.
- Oura's high retention suggests subscription fatigue is concentrated among WHOOP-style performance users rather than Oura-style wellness users; Helio Strap's sell-out supports this.

### Gaps
- No survey found that quantifies WHOOP or Oura cancellation intent, churn rate, or the share of buyers who reject a device because of a subscription. Reddit r/whoop, r/ouraring and r/FitnessTrackers were not directly accessible; no poll data from those communities was found.
- No data on who bought Helio Strap or Polar Loop (age, gender, prior device).
- WHOOP churn is not disclosed; Sacra estimates were not retrievable.

## KQ5. Life situations that correlate with buying recovery wearables (new parents, shift workers, endurance amateurs, longevity/biohackers, women's-health/cycle tracking) and general owner profile

### Takeaway
US wearable ownership has reached 46–57% of adults (Rock Health 2025) and owners are consistently younger, higher-income, more educated and more urban. Cycle/pregnancy/perimenopause tracking is the best-documented life-stage driver (Oura built its female majority on it). New-parent and shift-worker segments are supported by academic wearable studies but no market-sizing survey. Longevity/biohacking consumers are estimated at ~45% female and concentrated at 35–55.

### Cited Findings
- Rock Health 2025 (N=8,000 US adults, Census-matched): 57% own at least one wearable or connected device; 46% own a wearable specifically (up from 13% in 2015); ~4 in 10 own a smartwatch; Gen Z ownership 66%; owners are younger, wealthier, more urban, healthier, more likely commercially insured **[survey]** — [Rock Health](https://rockhealth.com/insights/whats-your-score-insights-on-wearables-and-connected-devices-from-rock-healths-2025-consumer-adoption-survey/); [MD+DI summary](https://www.mddionline.com/wearable-medical-devices/wearables-have-won-over-consumers-now-they-re-coming-for-the-clinic); [Rock Health "Put a ring on it" (YoY adoption patterns)](https://rockhealth.com/insights/put-a-ring-on-it-understanding-consumers-year-over-year-wearable-adoption-patterns/)
- Pew (June 2019, older data): 21% of US adults regularly wear a smartwatch/fitness tracker; 31% of households earning $75K+ vs 12% under $30K; 25% of 18–49s; college grads higher **[survey, 2019]** — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2020/01/09/about-one-in-five-americans-use-a-smart-watch-or-fitness-tracker/)
- HINTS-based analysis of US wearable ownership 2020–2022 (JMIR 2024) exists; full text blocked **[survey/academic]** — [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56504)
- Women's health / cycle tracking: Oura's female majority (59% → possibly 72%) coincides with its women's-health roadmap (cycle-aware Readiness, Pregnancy Insights, Perimenopause Check-In, women's-health LLM); Oura published an analysis of >10,000 pregnancies tracked on-ring **[company]** — [Femtech Insider](https://femtechinsider.com/oura-study-maps-physiological-changes-across-10000-pregnancies-using-wearable-data/); [JMIR mHealth 2025 pregnancy trajectories](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e80213); [Oura women's health page](https://ouraring.com/womens-health)
- New parents: large-scale real-world wearable data show sleep deficits persist ≥1.5 years postpartum (time in bed still 15 min below pre-pregnancy at 1.5 yrs) **[academic]** — [eBioMedicine / The Lancet 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/ebiom/article/PIIS2352-3964(25)00084-2/fulltext); WHOOP-based perinatal study (n=18) tracked sleep/activity across pregnancy **[academic]** — [BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12884-024-06991-1)
- Shift workers: multiple 2025 studies use consumer wearables with nurses (Singapore feasibility cohort May–Dec 2025; Croatian study of 140 nurses Jan–Jul 2025) and conclude wearable sleep feedback "may complement staff well-being initiatives" for nurses who under-report sleep disruption **[academic]** — [JMIR Nursing 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/96912); [IJERPH shift-nurse sleep study](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph23010064)
- Longevity/biohacking: global biohacking market est. $22.5B (2025), wearables the dominant segment; "affluent millennials and Gen X aged 35–55" are the key spenders; women ~45% of biohacking consumers (up from 20–25% in early 2010s) **[estimate: market-research firms]** — [Astute Analytica via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/09/3234305/0/en/biohacking-market-to-reach-us-216-68-billion-by-2035-driven-by-metabolic-monitoring-wearable-diagnostics-and-personalized-health-optimization-astute-analytica.html); [Very Big Brain stats](https://verybigbrain.com/brain-statistics/biohacking-statistics/)
- WHOOP Advanced Labs 350,000-person waitlist (Sept 2025) is a hard signal of longevity/biomarker demand inside the WHOOP base **[company]** — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/whoop-expands-advanced-labs-offering-non-members-adds-grails-multi-cancer-detection)
- Endurance amateurs: see Strava Gen Z race participation and run-club growth in KQ4 **[company/survey]** — [Strava](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025)
- Insurance-incentive angle: ~70% of Americans say they would wear a tracker for a health-insurance discount **[survey: ValuePenguin/LendingTree]** — [ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/fitness-tracker-smartwatch-health-survey)

### Inferences
- Segment ranking by evidence strength for Meta targeting: (1) women 22–44 interested in cycle/fertility/perimenopause tracking (Oura's proven growth engine); (2) hybrid/HYROX/CrossFit men and women 25–44 currently on WHOOP or Garmin; (3) longevity-curious 35–55 affluent (Advanced Labs waitlist proves appetite); (4) endurance amateurs 18–34 via run clubs; (5) new parents and shift workers (documented need, no market sizing).
- Because 54% of US adults still own no wearable (Rock Health), and most owners have no subscription, a buy-once band's addressable pool is larger than the WHOOP+Oura installed base (~8M combined).

### Gaps

## KQ6. How wearable buyers respond to ads (video vs image, creator vs brand) and pre-launch purchase triggers (waitlist, reservation)

### Takeaway
Fitness-vertical Meta benchmarks favour creator/UGC vertical video for cold traffic (roughly 2x CTR and 20–30% lower CPA vs studio creative), with static images holding in retargeting and Reels offering the lowest CPM. For pre-launch, deposit-based reservations convert 3–5x better than free waitlists; hardware pre-launch email costs run $5–12 per qualified lead. No wearable-brand-specific ad-response study was found; WHOOP's 350K Advanced Labs waitlist and Helio Strap sell-outs are the category's demand signals.

### Cited Findings
- Fitness Meta ads 2026: creator-style, UGC-adjacent, motion-heavy ads outperform polished brand video; Instagram Reels has the lowest CPM in the fitness vertical and highest CTR ceiling for native vertical video; fitness brands report CPA $18–28 with UGC vs $24–40 with studio creative; static holds in retargeting **[estimate: ad-agency benchmark]** — [AdLibrary fitness benchmarks 2026](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ad-benchmarks-fitness-2026); [AdLibrary fitness ad examples](https://adlibrary.com/posts/best-fitness-meta-ads-examples)
- UGC vs polished creative CTR on cold audiences: 0.8–1.4% (UGC) vs 0.4–0.7% (studio) across DTC; fitness UGC with an immediate hook "easily doubles" average CTR **[estimate]** — [Finsi UGC benchmarks](https://www.finsi.ai/blog/ugc-ads-performance-benchmarks/); [Influee CTR benchmarks](https://influee.co/blog/good-ctr-facebook-ads); [Opascope on when UGC works](https://opascope.com/insights/ugc-ads/); [Needle static vs video](https://www.askneedle.com/blog/static-vs-video-ads-what-actually-converts-for-dtc-brands)
- Oura's own marketing leans on self-expression/lifestyle to reach Gen Z and millennials (rather than performance stats) **[press]** — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/how-oura-keys-into-self-expression-to-reach-gen-z-millennials/761593/)
- Waitlist landing pages: average 2–5% visitor-to-signup, good 8–20%, elite >25%; deposit-based waitlists convert 3–5x higher than free lists; deposit lists see 60–80% email open rates vs 15–25% for free lists; typical 3-tier structure (free email, $1 refundable reservation, paid VIP deposit) **[estimate: agency/tool benchmarks]** — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/waitlist-landing-page-examples-10-high-converting-pre-launch-designs-how-to-build-yours); [Blazon waitlist strategy](https://blazonagency.com/post/waitlist-strategy); [Prelaunch.com reservation deposits](https://help.prelaunch.com/en/articles/8263057-what-is-a-reservation-deposit); [LaunchList benchmark tool](https://getlaunchlist.com/tools/waitlist-benchmark)
- Hardware/consumer-tech pre-launch: $5–12 per qualified email; a $500K crowdfunding target needs 5,000–10,000 qualified leads, $2M+ needs 20,000–50,000 **[estimate: crowdfunding agency]** — [BoostYourCampaign $1 reservation funnel](https://www.boostyourcampaign.com/blog/kickstarter-reservation-funnel)
- WHOOP built a 350,000-person waitlist for Advanced Labs before opening it (Sept 2025) **[company]** — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/whoop-expands-advanced-labs-offering-non-members-adds-grails-multi-cancer-detection)
- Helio Strap supply could not meet Q3–Q4 2025 demand (demand signal for sub-$100 no-sub band) **[company]** — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-zepp-healths-q4-2025-revenue-surges-43-93CH-4561628)

### Inferences
- For a May 2027 launch, a $1 refundable reservation tier layered on a free waitlist is the best-supported mechanism for both list quality and later conversion; Reels/creator video should carry cold acquisition while static "no subscription, ever" comparison cards work for retargeting.
- Given Oura's success with lifestyle framing for women and WHOOP's data framing for men, creative should be split: lifestyle/sleep/cycle UGC for the female 22–44 set, strain/recovery/price-comparison UGC for the male 25–44 hybrid set.

### Gaps
- No published A/B or case-study data specific to wearable brands' Meta ads (WHOOP/Oura do not disclose creative performance).
- Waitlist and CPA benchmarks are agency/tool estimates, not audited data.
- No evidence found on how Helio Strap or Polar Loop were marketed at launch or their acquisition channels.
