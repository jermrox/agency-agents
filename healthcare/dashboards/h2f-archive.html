Officer burnout linked to a measurable epigenetic change in a brain-health gene

In 134 Brazilian public safety professionals, higher burnout scores tracked with lower DNA methylation at the BDNF exon IV promoter, independent of depression and anxiety, and female officers reported more burnout. BDNF supports neuroplasticity, so this is an early biological marker of chronic occupational stress. It is cross-sectional, but it adds weight to treating burnout as a physiological load, not a mood problem.

'Operator Syndrome' still lacks a definition, scoping review warns

A PRISMA-ScR review found only six sources (2020-2026) that explicitly define Operator Syndrome among U.S. special operations forces, and only two were empirical. The construct is consistently described as the combined burden of blast, repeated mild TBI, chronic musculoskeletal loading, sleep disruption and sustained stress. For SOF performance and medical teams, the takeaway is that the label is widely used but validated assessment tools and outcome data are still missing.

Meta-analysis confirms caffeine protects vigilance and task performance under sleep loss

Pooling seven double-blind placebo-controlled trials in military or military-like populations, this review found acute caffeine improved vigilance (SMD 0.73), complex cognition (SMD 0.54) and military task performance (SMD 0.45) during sleep deprivation or restriction. It is the first quantitative synthesis of a tool most units already use, and dietitians and performance coaches can cite it when building caffeine guidance for sustained operations.

DHA orders testosterone screening for men 30 and older and RED-S screening for all women at annual health assessments

The Defense Health Agency turned the Secretary of War's July 15 health and human performance memo into clinical protocol. Male service members 30 and older are now screened for low testosterone at the annual Periodic Health Assessment with three symptom questions and a lab draw (optional under 30). A result under 300 ng/dL triggers a confirmatory LC/MS test and workup, and when a BMI of 27 or more drives the low testosterone, a 5-10% weight-loss lifestyle program comes first or runs alongside replacement therapy. Women of any age are screened for RED-S (fatigue, MSK pain, cycles longer than 35 days), and women 35 and older for perimenopause and menopause symptoms that affect duty. Dietitians and performance staff should expect referrals, because the guidance names caloric deficit, sleep loss and training stress as reversible causes and says levels usually recover within 4-7 days of adequate fuel, 7-9 hours of sleep and training recovery.

Skipping full meals in basic training doubled injury odds in Swedish conscripts

In 225 Swedish conscripts (96 women) followed through five months of basic combat training, those who regularly ate less than full meal portions had lower baseline work capacity and higher odds of injury or overuse symptoms (OR 2.33) and missed training (OR 1.85). Pentagon makes two fitness tests a year, male-standard combat scoring and a 0.55 waist-to-height cap permanent policy

The Pentagon reissued its fitness and body-composition instruction, folding last year's Secretary of War memos into standing policy. Resilience training helps first responders with stress short term, but evidence is thin

A Campbell systematic review of 220 trials found proactive resilience programs for high-risk occupations improved resilience and stress in the short term (SMD about 0.4), with smaller effects on anxiety, depression and PTSD symptoms. Performance psychologists should set realistic expectations for resilience programming and build evaluation into any rollout.

This Sports Medicine study compares 28 brigades with H2F Performance Teams against 28 matched controls (over 1 million soldiers, FY2019-2023) using a difference-in-differences design. Waist-to-height ratio replaces height and weight tables across the joint force

This December 18, 2025 USW(P&R) memo sets the joint rule behind every service's new body-composition policy: from January 1, 2026, height/weight tables are out and waist-to-height ratio (WHtR) is in, checked twice a year with a ceiling below 0.55. Anyone at 0.55 or above gets a body-fat check, with service limits no stricter than 18%/26% and no looser than 26%/36% (men/women), and fails go to remedial programs and medical referral. High fitness scores can earn allowances but never excuse noncompliance; DoDI 1308.03 is due to be updated by June 30, 2026.
